# Analysis job storage (STA-57)

Migration `049_analysis_jobs.sql` adds the storage for bounded analytical work: analysis and export jobs pinned to a consistent boundary, their bounded result pages and manifests, analytical concurrency slots per space and per cell, and an as-of reconstruction index over record events by feed position. It is the first of five slices of STA-15. Bounded interactive aggregations ([STA-58](analysis-aggregations.md)) use its analytical slots; [snapshot jobs](#jobs-sta-59) with submission, status, cancellation, result retrieval and the jobs Worker step that runs them (STA-59) are below; [measurements, freshness and reconciliation](analysis-measurements.md) (STA-60) follow, then [transports](#transports-sta-61) (STA-61), which make aggregations and jobs reachable over HTTP, the CLI and MCP.

The [contract](../contracts/v1.md) requires complete exports and long analyses to use a pinned consistent watermark and a resumable job ID bound to authorization and placement generation, with results reauthorized at fetch. This schema stores that binding and enforces its invariants; the worker that reauthorizes each fetch is STA-59's.

## Tables

| Table | Contents | Mutability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `analysis_jobs` | One aggregate or export job over one collection: its [binding](#binding), state, checkpoint, progress counters, budgets, lease, attempts, failure code, expiry and state times | Binding, budgets and expiry never change; state moves forward only ([states](#states)); kept until erasure |
| `analysis_result_pages` | One bounded page of a job's result: page number, row and byte offsets, row count, content (at most 1 MiB), byte size and SHA-256 digest, plus the job's binding | Immutable; removed once the job is cancelled, failed or expired, or at erasure |
| `analysis_result_manifests` | A job's complete result: page count, rows, bytes and result digest, plus the job's binding | Immutable; removed like pages |
| `analysis_space_slots` | A space's analytical concurrency slots, each free or held by one job of the space until a time | Holder only; removed at erasure |
| `analysis_cell_slots` | A cell's analytical concurrency slots, each free or held by one job of a space on that cell until a time | Holder only; cell capacity, not space data: erasure releases the slots a space's jobs hold |

`record_event_feed` gains `record_id` and `revision` and the index `record_event_feed_as_of` ([as-of reconstruction](#as-of-reconstruction)).

## Binding

Every job row binds its space and collection, the requesting principal, credential and capability (`records:read` or `export:read`), the space policy version and placement generation it was admitted under, the collection's schema version, the watermark, the canonical spec and its SHA-256 digest. Every result page and manifest row carries the space, collection, job, credential, spec digest, schema version and watermark, and a foreign key to `analysis_jobs`' `analysis_jobs_result_binding` key requires them to equal the job's (`23503` otherwise). None of these change after admission (`analysis_jobs_binding`, `23514`); nor do the budgets or expiry.

Admission (the insert) checks, under share locks of the space and collection rows (the same locks record writers take, so a lifecycle, policy, placement or schema change waits for an admission in flight and an admission that meets one waits and is then checked against it):

- the space is `active` or `readOnly` and the collection is not deleted (`analysis_jobs_admission`);
- the policy version, placement generation and schema version are the current ones (`analysis_jobs_binding`);
- the principal owns the space, or the credential's current, unexpired grant on the collection includes the capability (`analysis_jobs_grant`); this is a storage fence on top of, not instead of, request authentication and fetch-time reauthorization;
- the watermark is a position of this collection's event feed, or 0 while the collection has none (`analysis_jobs_watermark`). Events still pending publication have no position yet and fall after any watermark, so the worker publishes the collection's pending events before it pins one. A watermark is a committed past boundary, not necessarily the latest: later publications may follow it, and 0 is the boundary before the collection's first published event. The check takes no lock, so a concurrent first publication or a stale snapshot can pass the zero check; that leaves 0 the boundary before that publication, as any position W is the boundary before W+1, and an as-of read at 0 stays empty;
- the digest is the SHA-256 of the spec's UTF-8 bytes (`analysis_jobs_spec_digest`) and the spec is a JSON object of at most 64 KiB (`analysis_jobs_spec`);
- the job starts `queued` with nothing done (`analysis_jobs_transition`), and its expiry is after its creation time, which the database stamps (`analysis_jobs_expiry`).

## States

```
queued ──> running ──> completed ──> expired
  │          │  ↺ (lease, progress)
  │          ├──> cancelling ──> cancelled
  │          │     │ ↺
  │          │     └──> expired
  │          ├──> failed
  │          └──> expired
  ├──> cancelled, failed or expired
```

`cancelled`, `failed` and `expired` are final; `cancelling` may still expire.

- A running job holds a lease (`lease_id`, `leased_until`); a lease ends no later than the job expires. A reclaim after an interruption keeps the job `running`, takes a new lease and counts an attempt; attempts change only into `running`.
- Only a running job progresses: its checkpoint (`cursor_record_id`, the last record finished in key order, and `checkpoint`, a JSON object of at most 1 MiB), `records_scanned` and `runtime_ms`. Counters never decrease and stay within `budget_records` and `budget_runtime_ms` (`analysis_jobs_budget`).
- A job runs, and completes, only before `expires_at`, and expires only after it (`analysis_jobs_expiry`). A completed job expires too: `expires_at` bounds its result's lifetime.
- A job completes only with its manifest (`analysis_jobs_manifest`); fails only with a `failure_code` (`analysis_jobs_failure`); a queued job is cancelled directly, a running one through `cancelling`.
- Each state stamps its own time (`started_at`, `cancel_requested_at`, `finished_at`). Ending a job (cancelled, completed, failed or expired) clears its lease and releases its slots.
- A job row is never deleted except by erasure (`analysis_jobs_immutable`).

## Results

A page is written by its running job, under an unexpired lease and before the job expires (`analysis_result_pages_job`); so is a manifest (`analysis_result_manifests_job`). Each write first updates its job row without changing it, so a job's page writes, its manifest and its cancelling, failing, expiring or completing take turns: one that meets another in flight waits for it and is then checked against it under `READ COMMITTED` (a page that meets a cancel or a manifest is refused; a manifest that meets a page no longer matches its pages), or fails with a serialization error (`40001`, retry the transaction) under `REPEATABLE READ` when the other committed after its snapshot. So a worker whose lease has lapsed is refused too. Page numbers and the primary key let only one of two workers write a given page; fencing a write to the exact lease is the worker's (STA-59), in the same transaction. Pages are numbered from 1 without gaps; each page's `row_offset` and `byte_offset` continue the previous page's (`analysis_result_pages_order`); the content is at most 1 MiB; `byte_size` is derived from it; a page holds at least one row and one byte (`analysis_result_pages_nonempty`) and a result has at most 10,000 pages (`analysis_result_pages_count`), so the pages a job writes and its manifest seals stay bounded whatever its byte budget; `content_sha256` must be its digest (`analysis_result_pages_digest`); and the job's result bytes stay within `budget_bytes` (`analysis_result_pages_budget`).

The manifest states the page count, total rows, total bytes and `result_sha256`, the SHA-256 digest of the pages' `content_sha256` values (lowercase hex) concatenated in page order; all four are checked against the pages (`analysis_result_manifests_totals`). Only a manifest marks a result complete, and once it exists no page can be added (`analysis_result_pages_sealed`), so pages without a manifest are never a complete result, and a committed manifest states exactly the job's committed pages. Pages and manifests never change (`*_immutable`) and are removed only once their job is cancelled, failed or expired, or by the purge routine (`*_kept`). The removal guard runs with the deleting role's rights, as the retrieval tables' does, so the erasing setting confers nothing on a role other than the table owner the purge routine runs as.

## Concurrency slots

Analytical work takes capacity from slots, apart from interactive reads. A job holds at most one space slot and one cell slot (`analysis_space_slots_one_per_job`, `analysis_cell_slots_one_per_job`). A slot's holder must be a job that has not ended, of the slot's space (by foreign key) or of a space on the slot's cell, held no later than the job expires (`*_holder`); a slot keeps its number (`*_key`). A slot is taken (given a new holder) only in an `active` or `readOnly` space, and a space slot is added only in one (`*_space`). Taking a slot writes its holder job's row without changing it, as a result write does, so a taking and the job's ending take turns at READ COMMITTED and REPEATABLE READ: a taking that meets an ending is refused (`*_holder`: an insert waits for the ending first, an update fails at once with `55P03`), or fails with `40001` when the ending committed after its snapshot; an ending that meets a taking waits and releases the slot, or fails with `40001` when the taking committed after its snapshot. An ended job holds no slot once it commits.

Lock a space, then its job, then their slots: a purge locks its space, its jobs and then their slots, and a job's ending locks its row and then the slots it holds. A slot added or taken by `INSERT`, including the insert of an upsert (`INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE`), share-locks the space and writes the job, waiting for each, before its slot row exists. A slot taken by `UPDATE`, including an upsert's update, already holds its slot row, so it waits for nothing more: it share-locks the space and locks the job without waiting, and is refused at once with `55P03`, to retry, while a purge or a lifecycle, policy, placement or cell change holds the space (`*_space`) or while a result write, progress, ending or another taking holds the job (`*_holder`). So no slot is taken while its space is being suspended, deleted or moved to another cell. Extending a hold (same holder) locks nothing more: an ending that meets it waits for the slot row and releases it, and an extension that meets a release finds the slot free. So a taking, upsert or extension racing another taking, an ending, a space change or a purge finishes in one order, and none aborts with a deadlock. A held slot passes to another job only once its `held_until` has passed (`*_held`); its holder may extend it, never past the job's expiry. A statement that needs no job, such as an interactive aggregation, holds a free slot's row lock for its transaction instead of naming a holder.

Slot numbers run from 1 to 10, a structural upper bound of half the largest `originConnectionLimit` the topology schema accepts (5 to 20, [regional topology](regional-topology.md#provider-regions-and-budget-2026-09-28)); the jobs Worker's consumers and tick already hold up to half of a cell's origin connections, leaving the rest to the API and MCP Workers ([durable outbox](durable-outbox.md)). The rows present set the capacity in use; no slot is seeded. STA-58 inserts the slots its configured limit allows, derived from the cell's configured origin limit ([interactive aggregations](analysis-aggregations.md#analytical-capacity)), and [analysis measurements](analysis-measurements.md#limits) (STA-60) record the limits with their evidence.

## As-of reconstruction

A record's state at a watermark is the after-image of its highest revision whose event is published at or below the watermark; a record whose first event is published later did not exist, and one whose highest such revision is a `delete` was deleted. Taking the highest revision rather than the highest position keeps the answer right even if two revisions of one record were ever published out of order (publication orders pending events by their commit clock).

Each feed row now carries its event's `record_id` and `revision`, derived from the event by trigger on every write whatever the publisher sends; a row naming another collection's event is refused (`record_event_feed_event`, `23503`). The index `record_event_feed_as_of` on `(space_id, collection_id, record_id, revision) INCLUDE (position)` serves the feed-side lookup as one backward index-only scan per record, which skips only the revisions published after the watermark and reads no event; the query then fetches only the selected revision's event:

```sql
SELECT r.record_id, e.revision, e.operation, e.canonical_data
FROM records r
CROSS JOIN LATERAL (SELECT f.revision FROM record_event_feed f
  WHERE f.space_id = r.space_id AND f.collection_id = r.collection_id AND f.record_id = r.record_id AND f.position <= $3
  ORDER BY f.revision DESC LIMIT 1) a
JOIN record_events e ON e.space_id = r.space_id AND e.collection_id = r.collection_id AND e.record_id = r.record_id
  AND e.revision = a.revision
WHERE r.space_id = $1 AND r.collection_id = $2 AND r.record_id > $4
ORDER BY r.record_id LIMIT $5
```

Records keep their tombstones until erasure, so keyset pages over `records` see every record that ever existed.

Measured on local PostgreSQL 17 (pgvector image) with 50,000 records, 200 of them updated 2,000 times after the watermark (450,000 events), warm cache, one run each:

| Lookup | 1,000-record page with 4 hot records | Only the 200 hot records |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before 049: `record_events` revisions descending, each probing the feed for its position | 45.7 ms, 40,567 buffer hits | 1,912 ms |
| `record_event_feed_as_of` (by revision, `INCLUDE (position)`) | 7.6 ms | 123 ms |
| An index by position instead (one descent, but wrong if revisions were published out of order) | 9.2 ms | not measured |

Before this index the cost of a record grew with the writes after the watermark at about 9.5 ms per 2,000 later revisions; with it, about 0.36 ms. Deriving the two columns on publication added about 16 microseconds per published event (10,000 events in 50-row publications: 285 ms with the trigger and index, 127 ms without the trigger), about 1.6 ms for a full 101-event publication. These are local observations, not Railway or Hyperdrive latency claims; [analysis measurements](analysis-measurements.md#snapshot-jobs) (STA-60) measures the job page read through this index, and external acceptance is to measure it on the target.

## Limits

These are initial protective bounds, not measured capacity: a spec of at most 64 KiB, a checkpoint of at most 1 MiB and a page of at most 1 MiB (the authority's own record bound), at most 10,000 pages per result, job IDs `anj_` plus 36 lowercase hexadecimal and hyphen characters, failure codes of uppercase letters and underscores, leases of up to 128 characters, and at most 10 slots of each kind. Budgets and expiry are per job; STA-58 and STA-59 choose their values, and [analysis measurements](analysis-measurements.md#limits) (STA-60) record them with evidence.

## Migration 049

The migrator commits the steps before 049 first, so 048's hold on `projection_generations` ends before 049 waits. The step takes `record_event_feed` exclusively first, under the migrator's five-second lock wait, then share-row-exclusive locks on `collections`, `collection_versions` and `spaces` without waiting. A space purge deletes record events, and so feed rows, before it deletes collections and updates its space, but an erasure repair updates its space before its purge reaches the feed, so the step never waits for a lock while it holds the feed: a purge already past its events holds the feed, the step waits for it, the purge finishes with its existing routine (no analysis rows exist yet) and the step then applies; a purge, repair or collection, schema version or space update still in flight on the other tables refuses the step at once. While the step runs, event publishers and readers, space purges and collection, schema version and space updates wait; record writers do not. A busy feed or table rolls the whole step back with `Analysis job upgrade waits for event feed readers, space purges and collection, schema or space writers; retry when long transactions finish`.

The step backfills every feed row's record and revision. Measured locally, it held its locks for 132 to 155 ms with 10,000 feed rows, 679 to 918 ms with 50,000 and 10.2 to 12.0 s with 500,000. The migrator takes the step's locks in its own order and, above 50,000 feed rows, refuses unless `STATEPLANE_POPULATED_INDEX_UPGRADE=drained` is set after stopping event readers and space purges, so an undrained upgrade holds them for under about a second. An undrained step's statements are also bounded to 15 s each (an initial protective value, over fifteen times the measured hold): one that runs longer rolls the whole step back, releasing the feed, and fails with `Analysis job upgrade ran past its 15 s statement bound and was rolled back, releasing the event feed; retry`. A drained upgrade is not bounded, since nothing waits behind it; only a role or database `statement_timeout` it inherits can end it, and that failure reports `Analysis job upgrade ran past the inherited statement_timeout (a drained upgrade installs no bound)` rather than the 15 s bound.

Down is transactional rollback: a failed or refused step leaves no analysis table, feed column, trigger or routine change behind, and a recorded step is skipped.

## Migration 050

Migration 050 builds `records_byte_order` on `records(space_id, collection_id, record_id COLLATE "C")`, the order export and aggregate job pages read records in, so each page starts at its checkpoint and stops at its limit instead of sorting the collection's records under a collation the primary key does not have. It is built with `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY` as the only statement of its file, outside any transaction and under the migrator's session lock, as 047 is: record writers keep running while it scans a populated table, and an interrupted build leaves an invalid index and 050 unrecorded, which the next run drops concurrently and builds again. The cost is one more index entry per record row version a write adds; STA-61 [measured](analysis-measurements.md#migration-050s-write-cost-sta-61) no difference in record write latency it could tell from run-to-run variation on a loaded host. Down is `DROP INDEX CONCURRENTLY records_byte_order`; jobs then still page in byte order, sorting each page's candidates.

## Erasure, grants, restore and upgrade

`stateplane_purge_space` now runs only under `READ COMMITTED` (otherwise `25000`, `a space purge runs under READ COMMITTED`); space deletion and erasure repair begin their purge transactions at `READ COMMITTED` whatever the cell session's default isolation, so it removes every analysis row committed before it locks the space rather than only those in an earlier snapshot. It locks the space's jobs right after the space (so a page, manifest or slot taking in flight finishes first and none starts after), releases the cell slots they hold, and removes the space's slots, pages, manifests and jobs before anything else. The space erasure report (`PostgresSpaces.erasure`) reads every analysis table, and the cell slots by holder, before it reports the database erased.

Run `pnpm db:grant-analysis` after migration 052 and after every restore, through the protected cell administration connection with `STATEPLANE_CELL_ADMIN_URL` and the inventory's `STATEPLANE_CELL_ROLE`. It grants the cell operational role `SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE` on `analysis_jobs`, `analysis_space_slots` and `analysis_cell_slots` and `SELECT, INSERT, DELETE` on `analysis_result_pages` and `analysis_result_manifests` and `SELECT, UPDATE, DELETE` on `analysis_removals` (migration 052's queue of expired results whose pages the jobs Worker step removes, filled by an owner-rights trigger), and reads each privilege back, so analysis cleanup needs no portability grant. Space deletion reads these tables to prove erasure, so without the grant the purge commits and deletion fails with `42501`. No grant allows deleting a job or a slot, or `TRUNCATE`; ending a job releases its slots through an owner-rights trigger. The checked digests use built-in routines, so no routine grant is needed.

`pnpm db:restore-smoke` seeds every analysis table (a completed export with a page and manifest, a running job holding a space slot and a cell slot), compares them across the dump and restore, proves the restored guards and as-of index, and has the restored operational role read each table for erasure and end the running job, which releases its slots. `pnpm db:upgrade-smoke` applies 049 to the 2,000-event upgrade fixture, checks every feed row's backfilled record and revision, plans the as-of lookup and the erasure lookups by index, and compares the upgraded schema with a fresh install. `test/integration/analysis-storage.test.js` (in `pnpm test:postgres`) covers admission and binding, transitions, results and their size bounds, slots, a cancel and a manifest racing a page write in either order under `READ COMMITTED` and `REPEATABLE READ`, slot takings by insert, update and upsert racing each other, endings, space changes and purges, a purge refused above `READ COMMITTED`, a spoofed erasing setting and a slot added after a purge, as-of reconstruction and its plan, erasure through a nonowner role, and a real upgrade from before 048 that commits 048 before 049 waits, rolls 049 back whole when the feed is busy, above its bound or slow past its statement bound (or, drained, past an inherited timeout, reported as such), is refused rather than wait while an erasure repair holds its space, and completes a purge already past its events.

These are local PostgreSQL checks. Railway Postgres acceptance of the migration, the grant script, the restore and the purge through the operational role remains external evidence.

## Jobs (STA-59)

`PostgresAnalysisJobs` (`packages/postgres/src/analysis-jobs.ts`) submits, reports, cancels and serves snapshot jobs; `PostgresAnalysisWorker` is the [jobs Worker step](#worker) that computes them. The application boundary exposes them as `StateplaneServices.analysis.submit`, `status`, `cancel` and `page`, which HTTP, the CLI and MCP [reach](#transports-sta-61) (STA-61).

| Operation | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `submit(actor, space, collection, kind, serialized?)` | `kind` is `export` (needs `export:read`, takes no spec) or `aggregate` (needs `records:read`; `serialized` is an [interactive aggregation](analysis-aggregations.md#spec) spec, parsed, admitted and checked against the declarations exactly as STA-58 does, after the collection is authorized). Returns the queued job's status. |
| `status(actor, space, collection, job)` | State, failure code, progress (records scanned, step time, claims, pages written), budgets, times, the [boundary](#consistent-boundary), its [freshness](analysis-measurements.md#freshness) (`exact` or `stale` against the collection now), and the manifest once, and only once, the result is complete. |
| `cancel(actor, space, collection, job)` | A queued job ends `cancelled`, a running one through `cancelling` to `cancelled`, in one transaction: its slots are released and its pages removed at once. A worker writing a page meanwhile finishes it first, then finds the job ended and stops. A job past its expiry ends `expired` instead; a completed or ended job is reported as it is. |
| `page(actor, space, collection, job, n)` | Page `n` (1 to the manifest's page count) of a complete, unexpired result, with the boundary and manifest. A job without a sealed result is `CONFLICT` (its state says why), a page outside the manifest `NOT_FOUND` (every page of an empty export: its page count is 0), a page number that is not an integer from 1 to 10,000 `INVALID_ARGUMENT`. |

### Consistent boundary

Submission pins the boundary every page and the manifest derive from. In one `READ COMMITTED` transaction that share-locks the space, collection and grant (`AuthorityTransaction.checkScope`), it publishes the collection's pending record events to its feed under the same advisory lock `events()` publishes under, takes the feed's last position as the watermark, and inserts the job with the policy version, placement generation, schema version, principal, credential and capability it was admitted under (migration 049's admission checks them again), then makes the final credential check described under [Authorization at every read](#authorization-at-every-read) before commit. One publishing statement sees every pending event committed before it, so every write committed before the submission is at or below the watermark and every later one above it, a multi-record write wholly on one side. A backlog of more than 5,000 unpublished events is published in committed rounds of 5,000, at most 20 per submission; past that the submission is refused with retryable `BACKPRESSURE` and the next one continues where it stopped.

The result is the collection's records as of the watermark ([as-of reconstruction](#as-of-reconstruction)): each record at its highest revision published at or below it, a record whose revision there is a delete excluded, a record first written later absent. It is never `updatedAt <= watermark` over current rows: a record replaced, patched or deleted after the watermark is reported as it was. The status and every page report the boundary: `{ spaceId, collectionId, watermark, records: 'as-of-watermark', schemaVersion, policyVersion, placementGeneration, capability, specDigest }`. The schema version is the collection's at submission; the records are as written then, under whatever version each was written. Each status, submission and page also reports `freshness`: whether anything was written to the collection after the watermark (published past it or awaiting publication) or its schema version changed since submission, read in the same statement ([freshness](analysis-measurements.md#freshness)).

### Results

- **Export**: NDJSON pages (`application/x-ndjson`), one line `{"id","revision","data"}` per record live at the watermark, in record ID order (UTF-8 byte order: keys are compared `COLLATE "C"` throughout, read through migration 050's index, so neither the order nor the page cuts depend on the database's default collation, which for example orders `a1` before `B2`), `data` the revision's canonical JSON. A page holds at most 1,000 records and 1 MiB; it is cut at the first record that does not fit. A collection with no record live at the watermark exports no page: the job completes with page count 0, rows 0, bytes 0 and the digest of no pages (`resultSha256` of the empty string), and page 1 is `NOT_FOUND`. Pages are cut over the records that existed at the watermark only (a record first written later is no candidate, so takes no place in a page and no part of the records-scanned count), so each page is a function of the watermark and the checkpoint it starts from, whatever is written meanwhile; reading past later records still costs the scan their index entries. A record whose line alone exceeds 1 MiB (data near the authority's own 1 MiB record bound) fails the job with `RESULT_TOO_LARGE` rather than be split or skipped.
- **Aggregate**: one JSON page (`application/json`) in STA-58's result shape, `groups` formatted by the same code, with `source.records: 'as-of-watermark'` and `snapshot: { scope: 'watermark', watermark }`. Each page of records is aggregated by STA-58's own statement (`statement` in `aggregations.ts`), with that page's records as of the watermark and their index values (derived from each revision's data, as the writer derives them, for the declared kind pinned at submission) in place of the `records` and `record_index_values` tables; the partial groups (counts, exact decimal sums, extremes by the statement's order, hour-bucket offsets) are merged into the checkpoint. A value the pinned kind does not take fails the job with `SCHEMA_CONFLICT`, more than `maxGroups` groups with `GROUP_LIMIT`, groups past 256 KiB with `RESULT_TOO_LARGE`; never a partial result.

The manifest (page count, rows, bytes, `resultSha256`, format) is written once no record is left and seals the result (migration 049 checks its totals against the pages); a job's pages are never presented as a result before it, `complete` is true only with it, and a cancelled, failed or expired job serves no page.

### Authorization at every read

Each `status`, `cancel` and `page` authorizes again: the directory and provider credential (`PostgresSpaces.scope`), the space, collection and grant locks for the job's own capability (`checkScope`) and, after the read, a final credential check inside the same transaction: the provider confirms the credential with its expiry, then, for a key, one statement against one clock reading confirms the regional key and its grant of the job's capability with theirs. That check is bounded as STA-58's recheck is (5 s, its provider call and statement together): past it the request fails with retryable `PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`, rolls back and gives its connection and scope locks back, and a provider answer arriving later runs no statement. Locks fence revocation, not time, so the reply (a page, a status, a cancellation's outcome or a submission's job) is released only if the earliest of those expiries still lies ahead once COMMIT is acknowledged; otherwise it is `FORBIDDEN` and nothing is returned (a cancellation or submission that committed stands; the worker refuses a job whose grant has since lapsed). So a revoked key reads nothing (`NOT_FOUND`), a grant without the job's capability `FORBIDDEN`, and a suspended or deleting space `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`, however complete the result already is. A job belongs to the principal that submitted it and, for an agent key, to that key: another key, or the owner, finds no such job (`NOT_FOUND`); any owner session reads the owner's jobs.

### Worker

The cell jobs Worker's once-a-minute tick runs `PostgresAnalysisWorker.step` last, for up to 5 s (or what the tick has left after the retrieval step's 1 s settle, at least 4 s), on its one connection; the upload reconciliation step gave up 5 of its 10 s for it, so the steps still total the 50 s tick budget. The analytical slots derive from the cell's origin connection limit (`STATEPLANE_ORIGIN_CONNECTION_LIMIT`, set by `scripts/topology.mjs`; without a valid one only this step fails, and says so with the tick's error). Each step:

Migration 052 (STA-76) adds the removal queue and turn record below; the step reads them, and moves expired pages to committed batches, once STA-77 lands. Until then an expired job's pages are removed in the transaction that ends it.

1. Ends jobs past their expiry, one per transaction, in a slice of its own (see below): `expired` releases their slots (migration 049's trigger) and removes their manifest; their pages, a completed result's included, are removed afterwards in committed batches, and none is read again.
2. Claims the next due job (queued, or running with a lapsed lease; least recently run first), in one transaction that share-locks the space and collection, checks the job's binding, then locks the job (`SKIP LOCKED`) and takes a slot of its cell and of its space until its lease ends (one it still holds, else a free or lapsed one, never one an interactive aggregation holds by row lock), adding missing slot rows up to the derived limits in a transaction of its own first if needed, and moves it to `running` with a new 30 s lease. No free cell slot ends the step; no free space slot passes that job over for the step.
3. Runs it page by page. Each page is one transaction bounded by the step's deadline that share-locks the space and collection, locks the job at its exact lease, extends the lease and slot holds (a job that no longer holds both stops), checks the binding, reads the next records after the checkpoint that existed at the watermark, each as of it (at most 1,000 records and, past the first, 1 MiB of data, by the as-of index), and commits the page (export) or merged groups (aggregate) with the checkpoint, records scanned and step time. With no record left it writes the manifest and the job completes. An interruption anywhere (a lost isolate, a cut statement, a crash before commit) rolls back that page only: the lease lapses, a later step reclaims the job (counting a claim), and continues from the last committed checkpoint, writing the same pages and manifest.
4. Gives the lease and slot holds back when it stops, so the next step, or an interactive aggregation, can take the slots at once.

The same step also writes owner [space exports](portability-export.md#worker) (STA-70). It first ends expired jobs of each kind in a slice of its own every step (a job of either kind ends by its state alone and its result goes in committed batches, an expired analysis job's pages highest first, the next job to clear read from migration 052's `analysis_removals` queue alone, so an unfinished pass continues only in what both kinds' turns leave), then probes each kind (bounded alone) for the wait of the job its claim takes next. A kind due alone takes the whole budget. With both due and time for a turn of each, the kind waiting longer goes first, stopping at its share (analysis three fifths, exports two fifths) but never so late that the other loses its turn; with time for one turn only, they alternate step by step whatever their waits, by the cell's turn record (migration 052's `snapshot_step_turn`), so each gets a turn within two steps whichever worker runs them. A job whose row, space or collection another transaction holds locked is not due (probes and claims pass it over), and a claim waits at most 100 ms for a lock. A failure of either never stops the other; the step throws it once both have run.

A binding that no longer authorizes the job fails it with `NOT_FOUND` (collection deleted), `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE` (space not active or read-only), `STALE_PLACEMENT` (placement generation changed) or `FORBIDDEN` (the submitting key revoked or expired, or its grant no longer including the capability; the space owner's jobs need no grant), and removes whatever it wrote. A job past its budgets fails with `BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. A page error that every retry would meet again ends the job failed too, its page undone and its slots released, instead of retrying it until expiry: a data or integrity violation or a program limit of the database (SQLSTATE classes 22, 23, 54) or a fault reconstructing the pinned spec fails it with `UNPROCESSABLE`. The job's clock is checked first, after undoing the page: a page its job's expiry overtook ends the job `expired` (slots released, pages removed), and one that outlived its lease (migration 049's `analysis_jobs_expiry`, `analysis_result_pages_job` or `analysis_result_manifests_job`, or any page error once the lease has lapsed) leaves the job `running` for a later claim, which continues from the last committed checkpoint. Neither fails the job. This holds at every step budget, the jobs Worker's 5 s included: the bounded session sends the rollback to the page's savepoint before anything else (no lowered `statement_timeout` ahead of it, which a transaction aborted by a failed statement would refuse with `25P02`), and bounds the statement after it again, since that rollback also undoes any bound set after the savepoint, whether the page error came from the database or from the worker. Any other error (a lost connection, a cut statement, a serialization failure) leaves the page to a later step.

### Budgets

Protective values; [analysis measurements](analysis-measurements.md#limits) (STA-60) records the evidence for each, and which one binds first:

| Budget | Value | On excess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Records scanned per job | 10,000,000 | `BUDGET_EXCEEDED` |
| Result bytes per job | 64 MiB (so a cancellation or failure removes its pages in one bounded transaction; expiry removes them in committed batches) | `BUDGET_EXCEEDED` |
| Step time per job, summed over pages | 600,000 ms | `BUDGET_EXCEEDED` |
| Lifetime, result retention included | 24 h from submission | `expired`; results removed |
| Unfinished jobs per space | 8 (submissions to a space take turns) | Submission `BACKPRESSURE`, retryable |
| Page | 1,000 records, 1 MiB; at most 10,000 pages | Next page; `RESULT_TOO_LARGE` past 10,000 |
| Aggregate checkpoint | 1 MiB of partial groups, measured as the stored `jsonb` text (as migration 049's bound measures it) | `RESULT_TOO_LARGE` |
| Lease | 30 s, extended by every page; a page starts only with at least 1 s of the step left | Lapses; reclaimed |
| Step | 5 s per tick in the jobs Worker (2 to 60 s accepted) | Next tick |
| Event publication at submission | 20 rounds of 5,000 | `BACKPRESSURE`, retryable |

Each page runs without parallel workers, so a job's work is one backend on its slot, apart from the interactive pools.

## Transports (STA-61)

Interactive aggregations and snapshot jobs are reachable over HTTP ([OpenAPI](../contracts/openapi.yaml)), the CLI (which speaks HTTP) and MCP through one application service each (`StateplaneServices.analysis`), so every transport admits, reauthorizes and refuses alike: the same result, or the same stable code and retryability.

| Operation | HTTP | CLI | MCP | Access |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Aggregate](analysis-aggregations.md) | `POST /v1/spaces/{spaceId}/collections/{collectionId}/aggregate`, body an `AggregationSpec` | `stateplane analysis aggregate --collection ID --data '<spec>'` | `analysis_aggregate` (the spec's fields as arguments) | `records:read`; a safe read |
| Submit a job | `POST .../jobs`, body `{"kind":"export"}` or `{"kind":"aggregate","spec":{...}}`; `202` | `stateplane analysis submit --collection ID --kind export` (or `--kind aggregate --data '<spec>'`) | `analysis_submit` | `export:read` or `records:read`; a write, not idempotent |
| Status | `GET .../jobs/{jobId}` | `stateplane analysis status --collection ID --job ID` | `analysis_status` | The submitting credential; a safe read |
| Cancel | `DELETE .../jobs/{jobId}` | `stateplane analysis cancel --collection ID --job ID` | `analysis_cancel` | The submitting credential; destructive, a repeat reports the ended job |
| Result page | `GET .../jobs/{jobId}/pages/{page}` | `stateplane analysis page --collection ID --job ID --page N` | `analysis_page` | The submitting credential; a safe read |

- **Admission before validation.** The caller is authenticated, then the collection and capability are authorized, before a spec's fields are checked: a caller without the space gets `NOT_FOUND` and one without the capability `FORBIDDEN`, whatever its spec's fields within the [body budgets](#transports-sta-61). Only the checks of the admission order below come first; they inspect nothing stored. A job's ID and page number are path selectors, so an invalid page number (not an integer from 1 to 10,000) is `INVALID_ARGUMENT` before the job is read.
- **One request on every transport.** One function decides what is refused before admission, `admitAggregation` and `admitAnalysisJob` in `@stateplane/contracts`: HTTP and MCP call it through `analysisAggregation` and `analysisSubmission` in `@stateplane/application`, and the CLI bundles it and calls it before it sends anything. It measures one representation, the request's compact JSON text with its literals kept (an overflowing number stays `1e400`, never the `null` `JSON.stringify` writes, and `-0` stays `-0`, as MCP's `serializeJson` writes them), and hands the service the spec in that form.
- **Bodies.** An aggregation spec is at most 32,768 bytes (`analysisBounds.specBytes`, the service's `specBytes`, the records query budget), alone or as a job request's `spec`, and a job request at most 33,792 (`requestBytes`, that spec in its envelope). A larger spec or request is `RATE_LIMITED`, not retryable, without `Retry-After`, for an aggregation and a job alike. Only the compact form counts, so whitespace, escapes and a number's spelling never decide a refusal: HTTP reads a body as sent, and the CLI accepts `--data` or `--file` as the HTTP body it stands for (the spec, or the spec in `{"kind":…,"spec":…}`), up to 262,144 bytes (`bodyBytes`, eight times the spec bound: room for a full job request with every character `\u`-escaped), and refuses more as `RATE_LIMITED` before parsing it. MCP arguments arrive parsed; a message carrying the same text is well within MCP's 4 MiB JSON-RPC bound, so up to `bodyBytes` as sent the same text is decided alike on every transport, while past it HTTP and the CLI refuse what MCP may still decide by its compact form (MCP refuses a message over 4 MiB `RATE_LIMITED`). The bound keeps what the host buffers and parses before authorization to 256 KiB. Measuring stops at the first byte past a budget, so its memory is bounded by the budget, whatever the request's nesting or length.
- **Nesting.** A spec holds at most 16 levels of objects and arrays, the spec itself counting one (`analysisBounds.specDepth`; a job request one more, its envelope). The deepest spec the grammar admits has 4 (the spec, `where`, a predicate, an `in` predicate's values), so 16 leaves room for the grammar to grow, while every recursive JSON encoder and decoder behind admission stays far within its stack: a 10,000-level spec overflowed MCP's `serializeJson` and the CLI's `JSON.stringify` at review. The bound is measured iteratively, so no depth exhausts the stack before it is refused.
- **Admission order.** After authentication and before the collection is authorized, every transport checks a request in this order: the body as sent within 262,144 bytes, HTTP and CLI (`RATE_LIMITED`); its syntax (text that does not parse is null, refused as not an object, `INVALID_ARGUMENT`); for a job, the request's compact size (`RATE_LIMITED`) and its envelope (`{kind, spec?}` and a known kind, else `INVALID_ARGUMENT`); then the spec, as an aggregation or a job's: its compact size (`RATE_LIMITED`), its nesting (`INVALID_ARGUMENT`), and that it is a JSON object holding only finite numbers (`INVALID_ARGUMENT`, not retryable). So a spec over both the size and the nesting bound is `RATE_LIMITED` either way. A literal that overflows, such as `1e400`, is refused, never sent on as `null`; since sizes come first, an oversized spec holding one is still `RATE_LIMITED`. Everything else in a spec is checked after admission, so a caller without the capability or the space learns nothing about it.
- **Reads and writes.** Aggregations, status and pages are safe reads: a statement cancellation is a retryable `RATE_LIMITED`, the CLI repeats a retryable failure as it repeats a `GET`, and nothing changes. A submission and a cancellation are writes: a statement cancellation during either is `COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, as for any write, and the CLI never repeats them. A cancellation is repeated safely (an ended job is reported as it is). A submission takes no idempotency key and its ID is lost with its response: submit again. The lost job runs to completion or expires 24 hours after submission, and until then counts toward the space's 8 unfinished jobs.
- **`CONFLICT`.** A page of a job without a sealed result (in any state but `completed`, or past its expiry) is `CONFLICT`, HTTP 409, not retryable: the status says why, and polling the status, not repeating the page, finds when a result is complete. `CONFLICT` became a stable v1 code in this slice; before it, transports reported it as a retryable `PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`.
- **Untrusted content.** A page's `content` is stored data (NDJSON of records, or the aggregate's JSON text), never instructions. MCP returns the same JSON as HTTP; nothing is left out, because the content is no more a model prompt than a record read is.
- **Analytical capacity in the host.** The local and Preview host (`app/src/lib/server/host.ts`) gives aggregations an analytical pool of its own, sized `analyticalSlots(limit).cell`, when `STATEPLANE_ORIGIN_CONNECTION_LIMIT` is an integer from 5 to 20 (the cell's origin connection limit, as for the jobs Worker); without it each authorized aggregation fails `PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`, not retryable, and jobs still run, on the jobs Worker.

### Limits of an export through the transports

An export result is at most 64 MiB of NDJSON (the job's byte budget) in pages of at most 1,000 records and 1 MiB, at most 10,000 pages; at the measured fixture's 123 bytes per record that is about 544,000 records ([limits](analysis-measurements.md#limits)), and a larger export fails `BUDGET_EXCEEDED` rather than be cut short. A client reads it page by page within the 24 hours the result is kept, each page reauthorized, and can check each page's content against its `sha256` and the manifest's `resultSha256`, the SHA-256 of the pages' digests concatenated in page order ([results](#results)). A page travels as a JSON string: JSON escaping at most doubles its 1 MiB (each `"`, `\` or line break becomes two characters), and an MCP result carries it twice, in `structuredContent` and in its JSON text, so a page is at most about 2 MiB in an HTTP body and about 6 MiB in an MCP result on the wire. No response bound applies; a deployed Worker is checked in external acceptance.
