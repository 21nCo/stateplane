# Space export: open versioned format and resumable owner export (STA-70)

An owner exports a whole space as an ordered set of parts sealed by a manifest, in an open, versioned format whose JSON Schema is [`contracts/portability-manifest.schema.json`](../contracts/portability-manifest.schema.json). It is the second of six slices of STA-17, after the [portability storage](portability-storage.md) (STA-69) it writes into. Import to a clean deployment and the retrieval rebuild are STA-71, erasure receipts and ledger replay STA-72, backups and restore drills STA-73, and HTTP, CLI and MCP transports STA-74. STA-70 adds the Postgres adapter (`PostgresPortabilityExports`, `packages/postgres/src/portability-exports.ts`), the application service `StateplaneServices.portability`, the export half of the jobs Worker's snapshot job step, and the format's verifier `verifyExportManifest` in `@stateplane/contracts`; none of these exists before STA-77.

STA-70 lands in two slices. STA-76 adds the format's JSON Schema, [migration 052](#migration-052) and its grants; the adapter, service, Worker step and verifier this page describes, and the export and verifier tests listed under [Evidence](#evidence), land with STA-77.

| Operation | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `submitExport(actor, space)` | The space owner's session only: an agent key is `FORBIDDEN` whatever it holds (`export:read` included), another account's session `NOT_FOUND`. Pins the [boundary](#consistent-boundary) and returns the queued export's status. A space with 2 unfinished exports or 4 held ones (unfinished, completed, or ended with parts a worker step has yet to remove), or more unpublished record events than 20 rounds of 5,000, is retryable `BACKPRESSURE`; so is a space whose writers do not let the boundary be pinned within the lock budget, and one held locked past the admission's bound (its 5 s statement bound or the admission time) while its access is still current; an admission whose access expired meanwhile is `FORBIDDEN`. A submission whose admission was sent but not acknowledged, or acknowledged after its access expired once a worker had claimed the export, fails with `COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` (never retryable) and the export's `jobId` (see below). |
| `exportStatus(actor, space, job)` | State, failure code, boundary (its `snapshotAt` to the microsecond, as stored), progress (current section, parts written, claims, step time), budgets, times (UTC to the microsecond, as stored: `createdAt` is the snapshot instant, `startedAt` the instant the export was admitted to run, shown once a worker has claimed it, as migration 051 stamps it at the first move to `running`), and the manifest's SHA-256 digest and part count once, and only once, the export is complete. |
| `cancelExport(actor, space, job)` | A queued or running export ends `cancelled`, so it is never read or run again, and returns at once: a worker step removes its parts in batches (see [Worker](#worker)), and the space counts it as held until the last is gone; a worker writing a part meanwhile finishes it, then finds the export ended. One past its expiry ends `expired`. A completed or ended export is reported as it is. |
| `exportPart(actor, space, job, n)` | Part `n` (1 to the part count; the last is the manifest) of a complete, unexpired export, with the manifest digest. Without a manifest `CONFLICT`, outside the part count `NOT_FOUND`, not an integer from 1 to 4,096 `INVALID_ARGUMENT`. |

Every call authorizes again: the directory and the owner's provider credential, the space share-locked under the request's policy version and placement generation (a change is `FORBIDDEN`, a suspended or deleting space `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`), and after the read the same bounded final credential check STA-59's job reads make, whose expiry must still lie ahead once COMMIT is acknowledged ([analysis jobs](analysis-jobs.md#authorization-at-every-read)). Submission makes that check in a transaction of its own right after the one that pins the boundary, so writers never wait on the provider, and admits the export in a third. The first transaction inserts the export `queued`, which no worker ever runs. The second makes the final check. Only once its COMMIT is acknowledged with the access it relied on still unexpired does the third admit the export, bounded by that same access: it is never sent once the access has expired, and one whose acknowledgement does not arrive before then is reported as unknown (below). The admission share-locks the space first, so a submission counting the space's held exports waits for it, and it reads the 30 s admission time under that lock: an admission is counted by every count that commits after it. Admitted, the export is `running` with no attempt yet and its lease already ended, so it is due at once (its status still reads `queued` until a worker claims it). So no worker can claim an export before its submission's authorization is released. Once the admission's COMMIT is acknowledged, the access is checked again before the call returns: if it has expired meanwhile (an admission processed just before the expiry, or a process stalled past it), the export is cancelled while it still holds the admission's lease, so no worker has claimed it and it has no parts, and the call fails `FORBIDDEN`; if a worker claimed it first, or the cancellation cannot run, the call fails with `COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` and the `jobId`, and the export, which may run, stays the owner's, readable only through calls that authorize again. A success is never returned after the access expired. A refused check, or one whose access lapses before its COMMIT is acknowledged, fails the call and cancels the still-queued export before the call returns; it never ran and has no parts. The check changes nothing, so a check whose COMMIT is not acknowledged is a check that never answered: the export is cancelled the same way and the call fails with retryable `PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`. If that cancellation cannot run (the submitter stopped, or the database is unreachable), the call fails with both errors, and the export stays `queued`, unrun. An admission that fails before its COMMIT (its access expiring first, a lost connection, a statement error) surely rolled back: the export is cancelled the same way and the call fails with that error (`FORBIDDEN` for the expiry). If the admission's COMMIT was sent but not acknowledged, whether it was admitted is unknown: the call fails with `COMMIT_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN`, which is never retryable (submitting again would start another export), and an error whose `jobId` names the export, which the owner reads or cancels by that ID; never admitted, it is cancelled unrun. An export still `queued` 30 s after submission was never admitted: a worker step cancels it, and it no longer counts against the space's exports. An export belongs to the space's owner; any of the owner's sessions reads it.

## Consistent boundary

An export is one state of the space. Records are read as of a **watermark**, a position of the cell's record event feed, exactly as an STA-59 snapshot job reads one collection ([as-of reconstruction](analysis-jobs.md#as-of-reconstruction)). Everything else (schemas, retrieval policies, credentials and grants, sources, extractions, claims, provenance, links and evidence events) has no feed, so it is read as of the **snapshot instant**: the database clock reading migration 051 stamps as the export's `created_at`.

The two name the same state because submission pins both while the space's writes are held. In one `READ COMMITTED` transaction it locks the space row `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, which waits for every transaction holding the space's share lock and holds new ones until COMMIT (every authority writer and reader share-locks the space first: `AuthorityTransaction.checkScope`, the collection, source, claim and link writers, and credential issuance and revocation, which update the space row). It then publishes every collection's pending record events under the lock `events()` publishes under, takes the space's last feed position as the watermark, and inserts the export, whose `created_at` is stamped then. So:

- every write committed before submission is at or below the watermark and stamped (`clock_timestamp()`, the default of every timestamp an export compares) before the snapshot instant;
- every later write takes its share lock after submission commits, so its stamps come after the snapshot instant and its record events after the watermark.

Sections reconstruct the snapshot instant from those stamps: a row created after it is left out; a revision committed after it is left out; a source or claim deleted after it (its deletion event committed after it) is exported live; a link tombstoned after it is exported live at the revision before its tombstone; a credential activated, confirmed or revoked after it shows that time as `null`. The comparison is microsecond-exact (the instant is passed back as the database's own text). It relies on the database clock not stepping backwards while writers wait, as every timestamp comparison in the authority does. Holding writes costs at most the lock budget (`55P03` becomes retryable `BACKPRESSURE`) plus one round of event publication; the provider check is made after the hold ends.

Grants alone have no history: issuance adds them in a transaction after the credential row is stamped, and revocation deletes them. Every such change increments the space's policy version, so the `permissions` section is written first, and each of its parts checks, with the space share-locked, that the policy version is still the one the export was admitted under. A key issued or revoked (or another policy change while the space stays exportable) before the section is complete fails the export with `POLICY_CHANGED`, never a guessed grant; submit again. A suspension or deletion fails it with `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE` instead, at any section. Once the section is complete (the transaction that finds it exhausted moves on to the next section), later policy changes do not affect the export.

A placement change fails an export with `STALE_PLACEMENT`, a space no longer active or read-only with `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`, and an owner change with `FORBIDDEN`. Export is admitted into an active or read-only space (migration 051).

## Format (version 1)

An export is parts 1 to n. Parts 1 to n−1 are data parts; part n is the manifest. Each data part holds one section's NDJSON (UTF-8, one JSON object per line, `\n` after each) from where that section's previous part stopped. A part holds at most 1 MiB and never splits a character, but a line may continue into the next part, so no row is too large to export; a part's `rows` counts the lines that end in it. Concatenating a section's parts in order gives the section's NDJSON. Sections appear in this order, each only if it has rows:

| Section | One line per | As of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `permissions` | agent credential: its ID, principal, times and collection grants | created at or before the instant; later activation, confirmation or revocation shown as `null`; grants read under the submission's policy version |
| `schemas` | accepted collection definition version (`definition` is the canonical JSON as accepted) | accepted at or before the instant |
| `retrieval` | retrieval policy version (what is projected, never a projection) | created at or before the instant |
| `sources` | live source: locator, author, and its original's SHA-256 digest, size and type | live at the instant |
| `extractions` | extraction version of a live source, with its content and offsets | created at or before the instant |
| `claims` | revision of a live claim, with attribution, validity, review and commit time | committed at or before the instant |
| `provenance` | evidence span a claim revision cites: source, extraction version and offsets | as its claim revision |
| `links` | live link, its type, deletion policy and endpoints | live at the instant, at its revision there |
| `events` | evidence lifecycle event: a source's or claim's deletion, or a link's creation, deletion or detachment (migration 044 records no other) | committed at or before the instant |
| `records` | record live at the watermark: key mode and key, revision, schema version and canonical data | as of the watermark |
| `revisions` | record event published at or below the watermark, deletions included, with its record's key mode and key, its feed position and credential | at or below the watermark |

Every instant in every section is UTC RFC 3339 to the microsecond, exactly as the database stores it (`2026-11-13T01:02:03.123456Z`; the schema's `instant` pattern), never rounded through a millisecond clock; an instant the format cannot carry (an infinite timestamp) fails the export `UNPROCESSABLE`. A source or claim deleted at the instant is present only as its deletion event, never as content. A claim's provenance is exactly as stored: a whole-source citation has `extractionVersion`, `startOffset` and `endOffset` all `null`, a whole-extraction citation null offsets, and a span all three (offsets are null together and only with a version). A record's `keyMode` is `external` or `generated` and its `key` the normalized external key, or its own ID when generated; keys never change, so every revision line carries its record's, and a record deleted by the watermark keeps its external key (which stays reserved) in `revisions`. Original bytes never travel inside an export: a source states its original's digest and size, and an importer (STA-71) refuses an original whose bytes do not match them.

**Manifest.** The last part is one JSON object: `format` (`stateplane.space-export`), `formatVersion` (1), `jobId`, `space` (ID, owner principal, policy version and placement generation at submission), `boundary` (`watermark`, `snapshotAt` in UTC to the microsecond as written, any RFC 3339 date-time with a `T`, a `Z` or `+HH:MM` offset and no leap second as read (the database clock has none, so version 1 carries no second of 60), the instant every evidence section was compared with, `records: as-of-watermark`, `evidence: as-of-snapshot`), `sections` (every evidence section in order with its total rows and bytes, zero for an empty one, and `accelerator`), `parts` (each data part's number, section, rows, bytes and SHA-256 digest) and `excluded`. Its own SHA-256 digest is the export's: the job states it (`manifest.sha256`), migration 051 stores it, and the manifest part's own digest equals it.

**Verification.** `verifyExportManifest(manifestText, sha256, parts?)` accepts the manifest only if its bytes hash to the digest the job states, it satisfies the manifest schema in full (every required member, no other member in any object, each member's type, pattern and limit: the job ID, space, boundary, `excluded`, the 11 evidence sections once each and in order with `accelerator: false`, then optionally `embeddings` with `accelerator: true`, at most 4,095 data parts of 1 byte to 1 MiB, each of a listed section), and its parts are numbered without gaps, contiguous per section and total to its sections. Without the data parts it verifies the manifest alone. With them, each part must match the bytes, digest and rows the manifest lists (`rows` is the number of `\n` in the part, since a line may continue into the next part), each section's last part must end its last line, and every row must satisfy the one rule of its section no JSON Schema can state: a provenance span's `startOffset` is at most its `endOffset`. Then changing any byte of the manifest or of a listed part, dropping or reordering a part, or presenting a manifest re-sealed without a part fails. Rows are checked against the schema's section definitions with any JSON Schema 2020-12 validator. It uses only Web Crypto, so it runs in Workers, browsers and Node alike.

**Embeddings are accelerators only.** The schema defines an optional `embeddings` section (an embedding with its embedder, model, version, dimensions and the digest of the chunk it embeds), marked `accelerator: true`. It is never the only copy of anything: every chunk it could embed is rebuilt from the evidence sections, an importer may use a stored embedding only where the chunk it rebuilds has the same digest, and may ignore the section. A manifest without every evidence section is refused even if it carries embeddings. This exporter writes no accelerator section; STA-71 rebuilds projections from authority.

**Excluded material.** No export carries credential secrets or their hashes, session tokens, storage object keys, original bytes or derived projections, and the manifest says so (`excluded`). Credentials appear only as identifiers, principals, times and grants (no cell table holds a secret; the provider keeps them), and an importer reissues credentials rather than copying them ([principal map](portability-storage.md#principal-map)). Every object of the format is closed (`additionalProperties: false`), so a line with any other member is not a valid version 1 export.

## Worker

This section describes the export step as it arrives with STA-77; none of it runs at this head, where the jobs Worker step runs analysis jobs only and no export is ever claimed, written or ended by a worker.

The cell jobs Worker's snapshot step, `PostgresAnalysisWorker.step` ([analysis jobs](analysis-jobs.md#worker)), also writes exports, on the same connection and within the same budget (5 s, or less when the tick is nearly over, down to the 2 s minimum), never past it. An export is due once admitted (running with its lease ended, from admission or a lost lease); one past its expiry or never admitted within 30 s is ended by the step's expiry slice. Every step:

1. Ends expired jobs of each kind first, every step, each kind's expiry in a slice of its own (a tenth of the budget, and at most two fifths of the preparation time left for the first kind and two thirds of what is then left for the second, so both slices fit at the 2 s minimum, 120 ms each there when both are used up, and the probes keep the rest), whichever kind claims first, so a backlog of one kind never keeps the other's expired jobs (and their pages, parts and slots) in place. Each kind has one expiry pass per step, and a finished one is never repeated. An export ends in a transaction that changes its state only; its parts go afterwards in committed batches (below), so every slice makes progress however large the export, and one export's removal never holds up ending another. An analysis job likewise ends by its state (with its manifest) alone, and its pages go afterwards in committed batches of at most 16, highest first, the job with the fewest left first (migration 052's `analysis_removals` queue holds the expired jobs still holding pages, and the step chooses the next to clear from it alone). An unfinished pass of either kind therefore never makes its kind due: it continues only once both kinds' turns are over, in the time they leave, waiting at most 100 ms for a lock, so cleanup never takes either kind's turn or runs inside the other's window.
2. Probes each kind, each probe bounded alone at 250 ms (an eighth of the smallest budget), its connection checkout, lock waits (100 ms each) and execution together. A probe returns the wait of the job its kind's claim would take next, in the claim's own order (an analysis job queued since its submission before one whose lease ended; an admitted export since its admission or its lease's end). A job whose row, or whose space (or, for analysis, collection), another transaction holds locked, as another claim, a submission pinning its boundary, a lifecycle change or a purge does, is not due: probes and claims pass it over (`SKIP LOCKED`), so it never puts its kind first; an expired job is not claimable work and never makes its kind due. The expiry slices and probes all end at least 1.7 s before the budget does, so the first turn always has room for a page or part and its lease release, after the 100 ms kept for the turn record. A kind whose probe is locked out (its table held past the lock bound) or fails is not due this step, and a failed probe is reported with the step's failures. A kind left unprobed or whose probe is cut short is due with its wait unknown; an unfinished expiry pass does not make its kind due.
3. Runs the kinds due. A kind due alone takes the whole budget. Two kinds due share the step when the time preparation left holds a turn of each (each needs 1 s for a page or part, 0.5 s to give its lease back and 0.1 s to begin, so 3.2 s for both): the one waiting longer goes first, a kind with its wait unknown second, and the first stops at its share (analysis three fifths, exports two fifths), never before one turn and never so late that the other loses its turn; what the second leaves unused (nothing it could run, or a failure) goes back to the first. When the time left holds one turn only, the two alternate, whatever their waits: the cell's turn record (migration 052's one-row `snapshot_step_turn`) names the kind that goes first, which takes the whole budget, and the step names the other in the same statement (bounded at 100 ms), so the alternation holds across steps and ticks whichever worker runs them, a new one each tick included; the other kind gets what is left, all of it when the first finds nothing it can run. If the record cannot be read in time (or before `pnpm db:grant-portability`), the worker's own turn decides: the parity of the minute it was created in, then the kind its last such step put second. Each kind due gets a turn within two consecutive steps, whatever the other's backlog, lock contention or probe outcome. Preparation runs in the worker's own turn order, so neither kind's preparation always takes the other's slack. A claim also waits at most 100 ms for its job table, so a table held locked leaves its kind nothing to claim rather than taking the turn.

A failure of either kind (for example `42501` on `portability_jobs` before `pnpm db:grant-portability` has run) never stops the other: the step runs the other kind, then throws the first failure of each kind (an `AggregateError` if both failed), which the tick reports. An invalid origin connection limit fails analysis at every step and never stops exports. The export turn:

1. Ends exports past their expiry, one per transaction that changes the state only (found through migration 052's `portability_jobs_expiry` and `portability_jobs_queued`, by the transaction's start time, so never by reading the retained completed exports): `expired`, a completed export's included; and cancels exports still `queued` 30 s after submission (`unadmitted` in the step's result). A submission admitting its export holds it locked, and the step skips locked exports, so it never cancels one being admitted. Then it removes the parts of cancelled, failed and expired exports (`removed` in the step's result) in committed batches of at most 16 parts, highest first and one part per statement, stopping early enough to commit within the slice or turn, so the parts left stay numbered from 1 without gaps. Migration 052 queues every export that ends holding parts in `portability_removals`, by a trigger at the commit of the transaction that ends it, whichever path ends it (expiry, cancellation, a failure, a failed binding), with its parts left as its order (an analysis job cancelled or failed with its pages removed in that transaction is never queued); the batch that removes its last part takes it off the queue, and a space purge removes it with the export. The step chooses the next export to clear by reading that queue alone, never the parts or the retained exports, so the choice costs the same however many completed results the cell keeps (expired analysis results go the same way through `analysis_removals`). The export with the fewest parts left goes first and each gets at most one batch a round, so a small expired export in another space ends at once behind a large one, and the large one still gets a batch whenever the time holds more than one. An ended export is never readable, verifiable or claimable; it counts against its space's held exports until its last part is gone. Measured locally on PostgreSQL 17 (a laptop at load average 90), a batch of 16 parts of 1 MiB took 158 ms at the median, 316 ms at the 95th percentile and 404 ms at most, and a full 1 GiB export's 64 batches 10.9 s in all; at the 2 s minimum a 120 ms slice removes about 8 parts, so such an export goes within about 128 steps even when live work fills every turn, and far sooner at the 5 s deployed budget or in what the turns leave.
2. Claims the next admitted export, least recently run first (never a `queued` one), (passing over one whose space another transaction holds locked) in a transaction that share-locks the space (waiting at most 100 ms, else the export is busy until a later step) and checks the binding (placement generation, lifecycle, owner, and the policy version until permissions are complete), then locks the export (`SKIP LOCKED`) and moves it to `running` with a new 30 s lease. Exports take no analytical slot: the jobs Worker's one connection runs one part at a time.
3. Writes one part per transaction, bounded by the step's deadline: the space share-locked and the binding checked, the export locked at its exact lease and the lease extended, the next rows of the current section after the checkpoint read (at most 1,000 rows and about 1 MiB of lines), the part written, and the checkpoint (section, last whole row's key, bytes of the next row already written, step time) committed with it. A section with nothing left moves to the next in the same transaction; rows read but producing no line (a record deleted at the watermark) only advance the checkpoint. When no section has rows left, the manifest part is written and the export completes with its digest and part count (migration 051 checks the count).
4. Gives the lease back when it stops.

Reads are deterministic given the boundary and the checkpoint, so an interruption anywhere rolls back that part only, and a later claim continues from the last committed checkpoint and writes the same parts, digests and cuts. A binding that no longer holds fails the export (`STALE_PLACEMENT`, `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`, `FORBIDDEN`, `NOT_FOUND`, `POLICY_CHANGED`) and removes its parts. Part errors settle as STA-59's page errors do: a data, integrity or program-limit error fails the export `UNPROCESSABLE`; one its expiry overtook ends it `expired`; one that outlived its lease leaves it to a later claim; anything else leaves the part to a later step.

Export rows stay until their space is erased (migration 051), but the due probe, the claim and the expiry pass read only live exports (queued, running, or completed and unexpired), which the budgets bound per space, through migration 052's partial index `portability_jobs_live` (the expiry pass through `portability_jobs_expiry` and `portability_jobs_queued`). Measured locally on PostgreSQL 17 with 1,000,000 ended export rows across 10,000 spaces and 400 live ones (200 due): the probe took 1.0 ms at the median and 2.1 ms at most over 20 runs (1.8 ms on a new connection), the claim's selection 0.7 ms and the expiry selection 0.8 ms, all through the index; without it each scanned the table in 60 to 74 ms. The 250 ms probe bound leaves that two orders of magnitude of room. Submission counts its space's held exports the same way: live ones through `portability_jobs_live_space` (by space) and ended ones still holding parts in the space's entries of `portability_removals`, so a space's ended history never slows it. With 100,000 completed analysis results and 20,000 completed exports retained, each holding its page or part 1, every cleanup selection a step made, and the selection of the next job to end (2 buffers for analysis, which by `clock_timestamp()` could not bound its index range and read 5,638 buffers in 28 ms; an export's read 1,090 buffers in 6 ms), read at most 8 buffers in 0.01 to 0.04 ms (`EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)`, PostgreSQL 17 locally), while ended jobs were queued and once none was; the selection it replaces, which found ended exports by their part 1 among all exports holding parts, read about 100,370 buffers for the same history.

## Budgets

Initial protective values, not capacity, enforced by the adapter and Worker step that arrive with STA-77; at this head nothing submits or runs an export, and the runtime measured below is the STA-70 reference implementation's:

| Budget | Value | On excess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Part bytes | 1 MiB, migration 051's bound; a line continues into the next part | Next part |
| Rows read per part | 1,000 (and about 1 MiB of lines) | Next part |
| Parts per export | 4,096, the manifest included. The manifest's part list then takes about 581 KB (about 141 bytes per entry), inside the manifest's own 1 MiB bound | `RESULT_TOO_LARGE` |
| Part bytes per export | 1 GiB, the manifest included, held in the cell database until the export expires | `BUDGET_EXCEEDED`; an export whose manifest would pass it fails rather than seal |
| Step time per export | 3,600,000 ms, summed over parts | `BUDGET_EXCEEDED` |
| Lifetime, completed result included | 24 h from submission | `expired`; parts removed in batches by later steps |
| Unfinished exports per space | 2 (an export never admitted stops counting after 30 s) | Submission `BACKPRESSURE`, retryable |
| Held exports per space | 4, unfinished or completed, so a space holds at most 4 GiB of export parts (protective, not measured: the 1 GiB per-export budget times four); one stops counting only once its last part is removed, after it is cancelled, fails, or expires and a worker step ends it. An owner cannot release a completed export early | Submission `BACKPRESSURE`, retryable only once one of the four ends, at the latest when the oldest completed one expires (24 h) |
| Admission | 30 s from submission for its final credential check to be released and the export admitted (the check itself is bounded at 5 s) | Cancelled unrun by a worker step; a later admission attempt is retryable `BACKPRESSURE` |
| Lease | 30 s, extended by every part; a part starts only with at least 1 s of the step left | Lapses; reclaimed |
| Event publication at submission | 20 rounds of 5,000 events across the space's collections | `BACKPRESSURE`, retryable |

Measured locally on PostgreSQL 17 (Docker on an Apple-silicon laptop), one space of 20,000 records of 2 KB written in batches of 20 and not yet published, exported by the jobs Worker step at its 5 s budget: submission, publishing all 20,000 events in four committed rounds of 5,000 under the write hold, took 710 ms (so each hold lasted about 180 ms); the export wrote 88 parts and 88,662,385 bytes (records and revisions, about 1 MiB each) in 2,766 ms of step time over 2 steps, about 30 MiB per second of step time, with each part's insert taking 14 ms at the median and 25 ms at most. At that rate the 1 GiB byte budget is about 35 s of step time (7 ticks of a jobs Worker with nothing else due, 18 at the 2 s share when analysis jobs are due too), well inside the 3,600,000 ms runtime budget and the 24 h lifetime; the runtime budget instead bounds an export slowed by a loaded database. Exports of many small rows reach the part limit before the byte budget (1,000 rows of about 100 bytes make a 100 KB part), so the part limit, not bytes, is the first bound for such spaces. Production values against Railway Postgres through Hyperdrive are part of STA-77's [acceptance](#evidence).

## Migration 052

Migration 052 adds the live-export indexes, the two removal queues with the trigger that fills them, and the turn record. It locks `portability_jobs` and then `analysis_jobs` first, in a space purge's order, in the strongest mode it needs (`SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE`), so it never waits for one while holding the other, and builds nothing over the part or page tables, so it never holds them for a build: a transaction holding only those tables never waits for it. A part or page writer also updates its job row, so like any job-row writer it delays 052 by at most the lock bound below, and 052 then rolls back. Its four index builds read `portability_jobs`, which no writer filled before this slice. The migrator bounds its lock waits at 5 s and each of its statements at 15 s (an initial protective bound, not measured); a lock timeout, a deadlock or the statement bound rolls the whole step back with a message to retry once long export and analysis job transactions finish.
