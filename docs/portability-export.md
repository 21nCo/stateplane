# Space export: open versioned format and resumable owner export (STA-70)

An owner exports a whole space as an ordered set of parts sealed by a manifest, in an open, versioned format whose JSON Schema is [`contracts/portability-manifest.schema.json`](../contracts/portability-manifest.schema.json). It is the second of six slices of STA-17, after the [portability storage](portability-storage.md) (STA-69) it writes into. Import to a clean deployment and the retrieval rebuild are STA-71, erasure receipts and ledger replay STA-72, backups and restore drills STA-73, and HTTP, CLI and MCP transports STA-74. This slice adds the Postgres adapter (`PostgresPortabilityExports`, `packages/postgres/src/portability-exports.ts`), the application service `StateplaneServices.portability`, the export half of the jobs Worker's snapshot job step, and the format's verifier `verifyExportManifest` in `@stateplane/contracts`.

| Operation | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `submitExport(actor, space)` | The space owner's session only: an agent key is `FORBIDDEN` whatever it holds (`export:read` included), another account's session `NOT_FOUND`. Pins the [boundary](#consistent-boundary) and returns the queued export's status. A space with 2 unfinished exports or 4 held ones (unfinished, or completed and not yet expired), or more unpublished record events than 20 rounds of 5,000, is retryable `BACKPRESSURE`; so is a space whose writers do not let the boundary be pinned within the lock budget. A submission whose admission was sent but not acknowledged fails with `RECEIPT_PENDING` and the export's `jobId` (see below). |
| `exportStatus(actor, space, job)` | State, failure code, boundary (its `snapshotAt` to the microsecond, as stored), progress (current section, parts written, claims, step time), budgets, times (UTC to the microsecond, as stored: `createdAt` is the snapshot instant, `startedAt` the instant the export was admitted to run, shown once a worker has claimed it, as migration 051 stamps it at the first move to `running`), and the manifest's SHA-256 digest and part count once, and only once, the export is complete. |
| `cancelExport(actor, space, job)` | A queued or running export ends `cancelled` and its parts are removed in the same transaction; a worker writing a part meanwhile finishes it, then finds the export ended. One past its expiry ends `expired`. A completed or ended export is reported as it is. |
| `exportPart(actor, space, job, n)` | Part `n` (1 to the part count; the last is the manifest) of a complete, unexpired export, with the manifest digest. Without a manifest `CONFLICT`, outside the part count `NOT_FOUND`, not an integer from 1 to 4,096 `INVALID_ARGUMENT`. |

Every call authorizes again: the directory and the owner's provider credential, the space share-locked under the request's policy version and placement generation (a change is `FORBIDDEN`, a suspended or deleting space `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`), and after the read the same bounded final credential check STA-59's job reads make, whose expiry must still lie ahead once COMMIT is acknowledged ([analysis jobs](analysis-jobs.md#authorization-at-every-read)). Submission makes that check in a transaction of its own right after the one that pins the boundary, so writers never wait on the provider, and admits the export in a third. The first transaction inserts the export `queued`, which no worker ever runs. The second makes the final check. Only once its COMMIT is acknowledged with the access it relied on still unexpired does the third admit the export: `running` with no attempt yet and its lease already ended, so it is due at once (its status still reads `queued` until a worker claims it). So no worker can claim an export before its submission's authorization is released. A refused check, or one whose access lapses before its COMMIT is acknowledged, fails the call and cancels the still-queued export before the call returns; it never ran and has no parts. If that cancellation cannot run (the submitter stopped, or the database is unreachable), the call fails with both errors, and the export stays `queued`, unrun. If the admission's own acknowledgement is lost, the export was authorized but whether it was admitted is unknown: the call fails with `RECEIPT_PENDING` and an error whose `jobId` names the export, which the owner reads or cancels by that ID; never admitted, it is cancelled unrun. An export still `queued` 30 s after submission was never admitted: a worker step cancels it, and it no longer counts against the space's exports. An export belongs to the space's owner; any of the owner's sessions reads it.

## Consistent boundary

An export is one state of the space. Records are read as of a **watermark**, a position of the cell's record event feed, exactly as an STA-59 snapshot job reads one collection ([as-of reconstruction](analysis-jobs.md#as-of-reconstruction)). Everything else (schemas, retrieval policies, credentials and grants, sources, extractions, claims, provenance, links and evidence events) has no feed, so it is read as of the **snapshot instant**: the database clock reading migration 051 stamps as the export's `created_at`.

The two name the same state because submission pins both while the space's writes are held. In one `READ COMMITTED` transaction it locks the space row `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, which waits for every transaction holding the space's share lock and holds new ones until COMMIT (every authority writer and reader share-locks the space first: `AuthorityTransaction.checkScope`, the collection, source, claim and link writers, and credential issuance and revocation, which update the space row). It then publishes every collection's pending record events under the lock `events()` publishes under, takes the space's last feed position as the watermark, and inserts the export, whose `created_at` is stamped then. So:

- every write committed before submission is at or below the watermark and stamped (`clock_timestamp()`, the default of every timestamp an export compares) before the snapshot instant;
- every later write takes its share lock after submission commits, so its stamps come after the snapshot instant and its record events after the watermark.

Sections reconstruct the snapshot instant from those stamps: a row created after it is left out; a revision committed after it is left out; a source or claim deleted after it (its deletion event committed after it) is exported live; a link tombstoned after it is exported live at the revision before its tombstone; a credential activated, confirmed or revoked after it shows that time as `null`. The comparison is microsecond-exact (the instant is passed back as the database's own text). It relies on the database clock not stepping backwards while writers wait, as every timestamp comparison in the authority does. Holding writes costs at most the lock budget (`55P03` becomes retryable `BACKPRESSURE`) plus one round of event publication; the provider check is made after the hold ends.

Grants alone have no history: issuance adds them in a transaction after the credential row is stamped, and revocation deletes them. Every such change increments the space's policy version, so the `permissions` section is written first, and each of its parts checks, with the space share-locked, that the policy version is still the one the export was admitted under. A key issued or revoked (or another policy change while the space stays exportable) before the section is complete fails the export with `POLICY_CHANGED`, never a guessed grant; submit again. A suspension or deletion fails it with `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE` instead, at any section. Once the section is complete (the transaction that finds it exhausted moves on to the next section), later policy changes do not affect the export.

A placement change fails an export with `STALE_PLACEMENT`, a space no longer active or read-only with `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`, and an owner change with `FORBIDDEN`. Export is admitted into an active or read-only space (migration 051).

## Format (version 1)

An export is parts 1 to n. Parts 1 to n−1 are data parts; part n is the manifest. Each data part holds one section's NDJSON (UTF-8, one JSON object per line, `\n` after each) from where that section's previous part stopped. A part holds at most 1 MiB and never splits a character, but a line may continue into the next part, so no row is too large to export; a part's `rows` counts the lines that end in it. Concatenating a section's parts in order gives the section's NDJSON. Sections appear in this order, each only if it has rows:

| Section | One line per | As of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `permissions` | agent credential: its ID, principal, times and collection grants | created at or before the instant; later activation, confirmation or revocation shown as `null`; grants read under the submission's policy version |
| `schemas` | accepted collection definition version (`definition` is the canonical JSON as accepted) | accepted at or before the instant |
| `retrieval` | retrieval policy version (what is projected, never a projection) | created at or before the instant |
| `sources` | live source: locator, author, and its original's SHA-256 digest, size and type | live at the instant |
| `extractions` | extraction version of a live source, with its content and offsets | created at or before the instant |
| `claims` | revision of a live claim, with attribution, validity, review and commit time | committed at or before the instant |
| `provenance` | evidence span a claim revision cites: source, extraction version and offsets | as its claim revision |
| `links` | live link, its type, deletion policy and endpoints | live at the instant, at its revision there |
| `events` | evidence lifecycle event: a source's or claim's deletion, or a link's creation, deletion or detachment (migration 044 records no other) | committed at or before the instant |
| `records` | record live at the watermark: key mode and key, revision, schema version and canonical data | as of the watermark |
| `revisions` | record event published at or below the watermark, deletions included, with its record's key mode and key, its feed position and credential | at or below the watermark |

Every instant in every section is UTC RFC 3339 to the microsecond, exactly as the database stores it (`2026-11-13T01:02:03.123456Z`; the schema's `instant` pattern), never rounded through a millisecond clock; an instant the format cannot carry (an infinite timestamp) fails the export `UNPROCESSABLE`. A source or claim deleted at the instant is present only as its deletion event, never as content. A claim's provenance is exactly as stored: a whole-source citation has `extractionVersion`, `startOffset` and `endOffset` all `null`, a whole-extraction citation null offsets, and a span all three (offsets are null together and only with a version). A record's `keyMode` is `external` or `generated` and its `key` the normalized external key, or its own ID when generated; keys never change, so every revision line carries its record's, and a record deleted by the watermark keeps its external key (which stays reserved) in `revisions`. Original bytes never travel inside an export: a source states its original's digest and size, and an importer (STA-71) refuses an original whose bytes do not match them.

**Manifest.** The last part is one JSON object: `format` (`stateplane.space-export`), `formatVersion` (1), `jobId`, `space` (ID, owner principal, policy version and placement generation at submission), `boundary` (`watermark`, `snapshotAt` in UTC to the microsecond as written, any RFC 3339 date-time with a `T`, a `Z` or `+HH:MM` offset and no leap second as read (the database clock has none, so version 1 carries no second of 60), the instant every evidence section was compared with, `records: as-of-watermark`, `evidence: as-of-snapshot`), `sections` (every evidence section in order with its total rows and bytes, zero for an empty one, and `accelerator`), `parts` (each data part's number, section, rows, bytes and SHA-256 digest) and `excluded`. Its own SHA-256 digest is the export's: the job states it (`manifest.sha256`), migration 051 stores it, and the manifest part's own digest equals it.

**Verification.** `verifyExportManifest(manifestText, sha256, parts?)` accepts the manifest only if its bytes hash to the digest the job states, it satisfies the manifest schema in full (every required member, no other member in any object, each member's type, pattern and limit: the job ID, space, boundary, `excluded`, the 11 evidence sections once each and in order with `accelerator: false`, then optionally `embeddings` with `accelerator: true`, at most 4,095 data parts of 1 byte to 1 MiB, each of a listed section), and its parts are numbered without gaps, contiguous per section and total to its sections. Without the data parts it verifies the manifest alone. With them, each part must match the bytes, digest and rows the manifest lists (`rows` is the number of `\n` in the part, since a line may continue into the next part), each section's last part must end its last line, and every row must satisfy the one rule of its section no JSON Schema can state: a provenance span's `startOffset` is at most its `endOffset`. Then changing any byte of the manifest or of a listed part, dropping or reordering a part, or presenting a manifest re-sealed without a part fails. Rows are checked against the schema's section definitions with any JSON Schema 2020-12 validator. It uses only Web Crypto, so it runs in Workers, browsers and Node alike.

**Embeddings are accelerators only.** The schema defines an optional `embeddings` section (an embedding with its embedder, model, version, dimensions and the digest of the chunk it embeds), marked `accelerator: true`. It is never the only copy of anything: every chunk it could embed is rebuilt from the evidence sections, an importer may use a stored embedding only where the chunk it rebuilds has the same digest, and may ignore the section. A manifest without every evidence section is refused even if it carries embeddings. This exporter writes no accelerator section; STA-71 rebuilds projections from authority.

**Excluded material.** No export carries credential secrets or their hashes, session tokens, storage object keys, original bytes or derived projections, and the manifest says so (`excluded`). Credentials appear only as identifiers, principals, times and grants (no cell table holds a secret; the provider keeps them), and an importer reissues credentials rather than copying them ([principal map](portability-storage.md#principal-map)). Every object of the format is closed (`additionalProperties: false`), so a line with any other member is not a valid version 1 export.

## Worker

The cell jobs Worker's snapshot step, `PostgresAnalysisWorker.step` ([analysis jobs](analysis-jobs.md#worker)), also writes exports, on the same connection and within the same budget (5 s, or less when the tick is nearly over, down to the 2 s minimum), never past it. An export is due once admitted (running with its lease ended, from admission or a lost lease), past its expiry, or never admitted within 30 s. Every step:

1. Ends expired jobs of each kind first, each kind's expiry in a slice of its own (a tenth of the budget), whichever kind claims first, so a backlog of one kind never keeps the other's expired jobs (and their pages, parts and slots) in place.
2. Probes each kind, each probe bounded alone at 250 ms (an eighth of the smallest budget), its connection checkout, lock waits (100 ms each) and execution together. A probe returns the wait of the job its kind's claim would take next, in the claim's own order (an analysis job queued since its submission before one whose lease ended; an admitted export since its admission or its lease's end), or of an expired job since its expiry if earlier. A kind whose expiry ran out its slice (its table locked, or much to end), or whose probe is cut short or fails, is due with its wait unknown and goes after the other; a failure is reported by its own turn.
3. Runs the kinds due. A kind due alone takes the whole budget. With both due, the one waiting longer goes first: from a 5 s budget it stops at its share (analysis three fifths, exports two fifths, each at least 2 s) and the other takes the rest; below 5 s one share cannot hold a part or page of each (each needs 1 s plus 0.5 s to give its lease back), so the first takes the whole budget and the other what is left, all of it when the first finds nothing it can run.

Every turn ends with its job's lease given back, which moves that job's wait to now; an export whose claim finds its space locked past the 100 ms lock bound is passed over and its wait restarts too. So the other kind goes first at the next step: with both backlogged they alternate at the smallest budget and share every step from 5 s. A failure of either kind (for example `42501` on `portability_jobs` before `pnpm db:grant-portability` has run) never stops the other: the step runs the other kind, then throws the first failure of each kind (an `AggregateError` if both failed), which the tick reports. An invalid origin connection limit fails analysis at every step and never stops exports. The export turn:

1. Ends exports past their expiry, one per transaction: `expired`, parts removed, a completed export's included; and cancels exports still `queued` 30 s after submission (`unadmitted` in the step's result). A submission admitting its export holds it locked, and the step skips locked exports, so it never cancels one being admitted.
2. Claims the next admitted export, least recently run first (never a `queued` one), in a transaction that share-locks the space (waiting at most 100 ms; a space locked longer passes the export over) and checks the binding (placement generation, lifecycle, owner, and the policy version until permissions are complete), then locks the export (`SKIP LOCKED`) and moves it to `running` with a new 30 s lease. Exports take no analytical slot: the jobs Worker's one connection runs one part at a time.
3. Writes one part per transaction, bounded by the step's deadline: the space share-locked and the binding checked, the export locked at its exact lease and the lease extended, the next rows of the current section after the checkpoint read (at most 1,000 rows and about 1 MiB of lines), the part written, and the checkpoint (section, last whole row's key, bytes of the next row already written, step time) committed with it. A section with nothing left moves to the next in the same transaction; rows read but producing no line (a record deleted at the watermark) only advance the checkpoint. When no section has rows left, the manifest part is written and the export completes with its digest and part count (migration 051 checks the count).
4. Gives the lease back when it stops.

Reads are deterministic given the boundary and the checkpoint, so an interruption anywhere rolls back that part only, and a later claim continues from the last committed checkpoint and writes the same parts, digests and cuts. A binding that no longer holds fails the export (`STALE_PLACEMENT`, `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`, `FORBIDDEN`, `NOT_FOUND`, `POLICY_CHANGED`) and removes its parts. Part errors settle as STA-59's page errors do: a data, integrity or program-limit error fails the export `UNPROCESSABLE`; one its expiry overtook ends it `expired`; one that outlived its lease leaves it to a later claim; anything else leaves the part to a later step.

Export rows stay until their space is erased (migration 051), but the due probe, the claim and the expiry pass read only live exports (queued, running, or completed and unexpired), which the budgets bound per space, through migration 052's partial index `portability_jobs_live`. Measured locally on PostgreSQL 17 with 1,000,000 ended export rows across 10,000 spaces and 400 live ones (200 due): the probe took 1.0 ms at the median and 2.1 ms at most over 20 runs (1.8 ms on a new connection), the claim's selection 0.7 ms and the expiry selection 0.8 ms, all through the index; without it each scanned the table in 60 to 74 ms. The 250 ms probe bound leaves that two orders of magnitude of room.

## Budgets

Initial protective values, not capacity:

| Budget | Value | On excess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Part bytes | 1 MiB, migration 051's bound; a line continues into the next part | Next part |
| Rows read per part | 1,000 (and about 1 MiB of lines) | Next part |
| Parts per export | 4,096, the manifest included. The manifest's part list then takes about 581 KB (about 141 bytes per entry), inside the manifest's own 1 MiB bound | `RESULT_TOO_LARGE` |
| Part bytes per export | 1 GiB, the manifest included, held in the cell database until the export expires | `BUDGET_EXCEEDED`; an export whose manifest would pass it fails rather than seal |
| Step time per export | 3,600,000 ms, summed over parts | `BUDGET_EXCEEDED` |
| Lifetime, completed result included | 24 h from submission | `expired`; parts removed |
| Unfinished exports per space | 2 (an export never admitted stops counting after 30 s) | Submission `BACKPRESSURE`, retryable |
| Held exports per space | 4, unfinished or completed and unexpired, so a space holds at most 4 GiB of export parts; one stops counting once it expires, is cancelled or fails | Submission `BACKPRESSURE`, retryable once one expires |
| Admission | 30 s from submission for its final credential check to be released and the export admitted (the check itself is bounded at 5 s) | Cancelled unrun by a worker step; a later admission attempt is retryable `BACKPRESSURE` |
| Lease | 30 s, extended by every part; a part starts only with at least 1 s of the step left | Lapses; reclaimed |
| Event publication at submission | 20 rounds of 5,000 events across the space's collections | `BACKPRESSURE`, retryable |

Measured locally on PostgreSQL 17 (Docker on an Apple-silicon laptop), one space of 20,000 records of 2 KB written in batches of 20 and not yet published, exported by the jobs Worker step at its 5 s budget: submission, publishing all 20,000 events in four committed rounds of 5,000 under the write hold, took 710 ms (so each hold lasted about 180 ms); the export wrote 88 parts and 88,662,385 bytes (records and revisions, about 1 MiB each) in 2,766 ms of step time over 2 steps, about 30 MiB per second of step time, with each part's insert taking 14 ms at the median and 25 ms at most. At that rate the 1 GiB byte budget is about 35 s of step time (7 ticks of a jobs Worker with nothing else due, 18 at the 2 s share when analysis jobs are due too), well inside the 3,600,000 ms runtime budget and the 24 h lifetime; the runtime budget instead bounds an export slowed by a loaded database. Exports of many small rows reach the part limit before the byte budget (1,000 rows of about 100 bytes make a 100 KB part), so the part limit, not bytes, is the first bound for such spaces. Production values against Railway Postgres through Hyperdrive are part of this slice's [acceptance](#evidence).

## Evidence

`test/integration/portability-export.test.js` (in `pnpm test:postgres`) checks, against what the test wrote rather than what the worker read:

- a real export of a space with records created, replaced and deleted in two collections (generated keys, a live external key and one held by a deleted record), a source with an extraction, a claim citing a span, a whole extraction version and a whole source, links and agent keys, followed after submission by record writes, a record deletion that detaches a link, a claim review, a new claim, a source deletion, a new source and a new key: every section holds the state at the boundary only (each record's key mode and key, deleted ones in `revisions`; provenance `null` exactly where the citation has no version or offsets), every line and the manifest match the JSON Schema, the boundary instant in the manifest and status is the stored one to the microsecond, and every instant of every section (a link stamped with sub-millisecond digits among them) equals the stored microsecond text, and the manifest verifies against the digest the job states, with every part;
- the same manifest with one byte changed fails against its digest, a changed part fails against the manifest, a missing part fails, and a manifest re-sealed without a part's entry fails for its structure;
- on a real export with a key that wrote records and a revoked key, neither key secret, the owner's session token, the source's storage object key, nor any 16-character fragment of them appears in any part or the manifest, while the credentials' identifiers do; no member anywhere is named for secret material;
- an export of records near 1 MiB of two-byte characters, cut once at a checkpoint and twice at part writes, resumes and writes the same parts, digests and cuts as one run straight through, with lines continuing across parts and no part over 1 MiB;
- only the owner exports or reads (an agent key with `export:read` is refused), cancellation removes parts (a running export's already written ones too, and no later step writes more), the part bounds and states, a key issued before permissions are read fails the export with `POLICY_CHANGED` while a key revoked after they are read does not, a read-only space exports, and a suspended space fails a queued export and serves nothing;
- a submission whose final check commits but whose access lapses before the acknowledgement is refused, and worker steps run while it waits claim nothing; its export ends cancelled with no parts and nothing completes; a submission whose admission's acknowledgement is lost fails with `RECEIPT_PENDING` naming the export, which the owner reads and cancels by that ID;
- a space with 4 completed exports refuses a fifth until one expires and a step ends it; a byte budget of exactly an export's parts and manifest seals it, one byte less fails it `BUDGET_EXCEEDED` at its manifest with its parts removed;
- a writer holding the space's share lock delays submission until it commits, and its write is inside the boundary;
- with an analysis job and an export both backlogged at the 2 s minimum budget, the steps alternate (the longer-waiting kind first), and a failed export probe (`42501`) or a failing export part is reported by the step while analysis jobs still complete in it; with `portability_jobs` held `ACCESS EXCLUSIVE` a step at the 2 s minimum still claims and advances an analysis job, and with `analysis_jobs` held an export still writes parts;
- at the 2 s minimum, with an analysis job whose lease ended long ago and a fresh one queued before each step, an export admitted between them goes first (the probe follows the claim, which takes the queued job); an export whose space is locked is passed over at once, analysis takes that step and the next; with an expired analysis job and an expired export whose part removal fails, the analysis job ends in that step (pages and slots released), another analysis job writes a page, and the failure is reported; and a probe of either kind delayed 1.5 s is cut at its bound while the other kind writes in that step, which ends within its budget.

`test/portability-manifest.test.js` (in `pnpm test`) checks that `verifyExportManifest` refuses every correctly hashed manifest the schema refuses (a missing `jobId`, `space` or `excluded`, an extra member in any object, a malformed job ID, identifier, version, instant (a day its month lacks, an hour, minute, second or offset out of range, any leap second, announced or not, a space separator or an offset without a colon) or `excluded` item, a part of 0 bytes or over 1 MiB, more than 4,095 data parts); that the schema and the verifier both refuse sections reordered, repeated, missing or wrongly marked as accelerators and a part of no export section, and the schema a detachment with a credential; the rules the schema cannot state; and, with the data parts, a part holding more rows than it lists, a section ending in an unfinished line and a reversed provenance span, while a line continuing across parts verifies. `test/integration/analysis-jobs.test.js` still passes unchanged with the shared step.

These are local PostgreSQL 17 checks. Exact-head acceptance still needs Railway Postgres: migrations through 051 on a disposable cell database, `pnpm db:grant-portability` against the operational role, and this suite's real export run there (manifest digest verification and the credential exclusion check against an export written under the operational role, not the migration owner). The jobs Worker's cron advancing an export on Cloudflare Preview through Hyperdrive belongs with the transports (STA-74), which make an export reachable from outside the process. No connected provider sandbox or browser path changes in this slice.
