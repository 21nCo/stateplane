# Analysis measurements, freshness and reconciliation (STA-60)

The fourth of five slices of STA-15, after [analysis job storage](analysis-jobs.md) (STA-57), [interactive aggregations](analysis-aggregations.md) (STA-58) and [snapshot jobs](analysis-jobs.md#jobs-sta-59) (STA-59). It backs the expensive analytical access patterns with evidence and labels every result exact or stale:

- `scripts/analysis-measure.mjs` (`pnpm analysis:measure`, which builds first and prints the build on stdout; to keep a report, build and redirect `node scripts/analysis-measure.mjs ... > report.json`, as under [Evidence](#evidence)) measures aggregations, their plans, a candidate typed index, snapshot jobs, reconciliation and mixed interactive and analytical load on a named generic fixture. Its local report is [`docs/measurements/analysis-measurements-local.json`](measurements/analysis-measurements-local.json); [`docs/measurements/analysis-reconciliation-local.json`](measurements/analysis-reconciliation-local.json) measures the reconciliation alone (`--reconciliation-only`) with the validating reconciliation described below.
- The measurements decide what is added: [no typed index, projection table or partition](#typed-indexes-and-projections) is justified by them; the [limits](#limits) are recorded with their evidence, and [off-ramp criteria](#off-ramp-to-a-dedicated-analytical-store) say when a projection or a dedicated analytical store becomes justified.
- Every aggregation and snapshot job result states its [freshness](#freshness): `exact`, or `stale` once the collection was written after the job's watermark (published or awaiting publication) or its schema version changed.
- `reconcileIndexes` (`pnpm db:reconcile-indexes`) [reconciles](#reconciliation) the typed index projection that every exact filter, sort and aggregation over a declared field reads (an unfiltered count reads no index values) against what each record's canonical data derives, and reports drift.

HTTP, the CLI and MCP reach aggregations and jobs through the same services ([transports](analysis-jobs.md#transports-sta-61), STA-61).

## Fixture and method

`analysis-fixture/v1` is generic: each record holds `team` (a string, 20 values), `score` (a number, explicit null in every 11th record), `active` (a boolean, missing from every 13th) and `at` (a date-time every 97 s from 2020-01-01T00:00:00Z), all four declared filterable and backfilled ready. The measured collections (50,000, 200,000 and 500,000 records) share one space; another space holds 1,000,000 records of the same shape, so a measured collection is a fraction of the cell's `records` and `record_index_values`, as in a cell holding many spaces. The script seeds them directly in SQL as the writers store them: records, their typed index rows and, for the measured collections, their create events published to the feed. Reconciling the largest collection afterwards finds no drift, which checks the seed against what the writers accept and derive.

Each [aggregation shape](#interactive-aggregations) runs three times through `PostgresAggregations` with its statement budget raised to its 15,000 ms maximum, so a shape past the 5,000 ms default is still measured, on an analytical pool that records the statement as issued. Its plan is that statement replayed under `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` with the transaction's own settings (no parallel workers). Latency is the service's, admission included; the plan's time is the statement's.

The committed report ran on PostgreSQL 17.11 (pgvector image) in Docker on an 8-CPU Apple Silicon host shared with other work, at a load average of 7.6 at the start and 6.0 at the end. An earlier run of the same script, settings and fixture on the same host under a load average of 17.0 to 19.2 (28.5 over the last 15 minutes), without the last mixed load phase, is quoted as the *loaded run* where it differs materially: [`analysis-measurements-local-loaded.json`](measurements/analysis-measurements-local-loaded.json). A third run after the [mixed load](#mixed-load) page count was corrected, the *recount run* ([`analysis-measurements-local-recount.json`](measurements/analysis-measurements-local-recount.json): same script and settings, host load 7.6 at the start and 30.8 at the end, one-minute averages as for the other runs), is cited for job progress under mixed load only; its latencies are the host's load. The runs show how far the numbers move with the host's load. They measure this fixture on this server and set no product guarantee. The target, Railway Postgres, is measured by the same script with `DATABASE_URL` in external acceptance; the script connects as every operator script does (loopback without TLS, any other host only with `sslmode=verify-full`, checked before it connects).

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| `byValue`: by `team`, count, sum and average of `score` | 106 (112) | 566 (554) | 2,152 (2,156) | 4.30 (8.40) | Live records hash-joined with a sequential scan of `record_index_values` (214,116 buffers read) |
| `byMonth`: month buckets of `at` in America/New_York, count and maximum | 233 (240) | 1,046 (1,146) | 3,028 (3,453) | 6.06 (13.11) | As `byValue` |
| `threeDimensions`: by `team`, `active` and year of `at`, count and minimum | 378 (389) | 1,668 (1,829) | 5,805 (5,574) | 11.61 (19.55) | As `byValue`, three joins (319,302 buffers read) |
| `filteredBroad`: `team` equals one of 20 values, by `active` | 41 (27) | 218 (172) | 535 (480) | 1.07 (3.07) | From `record_index_string`, each match's `active` by primary key |
| `filteredSelective`: `score` below 1,000, count and sum | 18 (9) | 19 (9) | 21 (10) | — | From `record_index_number`: 909 matches at every size |

- An unfiltered aggregation costs time in proportion to the collection's live records, and most of it is the per-record grouping work (bucketing an instant in a zone, three-key groups), not reading: the shapes over the same rows differ 2.7-fold. At 500,000 records `threeDimensions` is past the 5,000 ms statement budget (in the loaded run `byMonth` too, at 6,554 ms); every other shape answered within it.
- At 50,000 and 200,000 records, where the measured collection is a small share of `record_index_values`, the plan reads each field's rows through the table's primary key, merge- or hash-joined with the live records. At 500,000, 29% of the table's rows, the planner switches to a sequential scan of the whole table, the other space's rows included.
- A predicate starts the plan from its typed value index and costs what it matches: `filteredSelective` stays near 10 ms of statement time at every size. A filter is how an interactive aggregation stays within budget on a large collection.
- Service latency adds admission (directory, scope and declarations on the interactive pool) and the slot transaction; at small sizes it is dominated by them, and on a loaded host by the load (in the loaded run `count` took 164 ms at 50,000 records, longer than at 200,000).

## Typed indexes and projections

The candidate is the one typed index the plans point at: each declared field's rows of a collection in record order, with their values, so each aggregated field is one index-only range instead of primary key lookups interleaved with the other fields' rows, or a scan of every space's rows:

```sql
CREATE INDEX record_index_by_field ON record_index_values(space_id,collection_id,field_name,record_id)
  INCLUDE (value_kind,string_value,number_value,boolean_value,time_value)
```

The script measures every shape with and without it at each size (built, measured, dropped), its build time and size, and record writes through the real write path in alternating rounds without and with it. Speedup is the median without it over the median with it:

| Shape | 50,000 | 200,000 | 500,000 | 500,000, loaded run |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `count` | 1.54 | 1.02 | 0.98 | 0.90 |
| `byValue` | 1.47 | 1.57 | 2.25 | 2.10 |
| `byMonth` | 1.02 | 1.21 | 1.32 | 1.83 |
| `threeDimensions` | 1.23 | 1.15 | 1.44 | 1.97 |
| `filteredBroad` | 0.70 | 1.58 | 1.11 | 1.52 |
| `filteredSelective` | 0.89 | 0.88 | 1.10 | 0.79 |

`count` reads no index values and its plan is the same either way, so its ratios (0.98 to 1.54 here, 0.90 to 3.00 in the loaded run) are the host's noise, the noise floor of every ratio here. At 500,000 records the index replaces the whole-table scan (buffers read for `byValue`: 214,116 down to 26,990) and brings `byValue` from 2,152 to 958 ms and `threeDimensions` from 5,805 to 4,043 ms, within the budget (loaded run: 9,777 to 4,975 ms). Its build took 13.4 to 22.2 s without `CONCURRENTLY` (loaded run: 17.5 to 35.4 s). It is 867 MB on 7.0 million rows: as large as the table itself (879 MB), and about half the size of its eight existing indexes together (1,785 MB), so it would grow the table's index footprint by about half. Record writes showed no cost from it at this resolution (p50 9.6 and 9.4 ms without it, 9.4 and 9.4 ms with it; p95 12.6 and 14.3 without, 12.8 and 12.8 with; 300 writes each, alternating; loaded run p50: 124.0 and 81.9 without, 111.9 and 74.6 with).

**Not added.** The gain is large only where one collection holds a large share of the cell's index rows, which turns the plan into a scan of the whole table; where it holds a small share, as in a cell holding many spaces, the index gains the unfiltered shapes 1.0 to 1.6 times, within the noise floor. Even at best it moves the slowest shape's ceiling from about 431,000 to about 620,000 records (loaded run: 256,000 to 500,000), while snapshot jobs already answer every collection up to their budgets and a filter answers selectively at any size. For that it would double the footprint of the cell's busiest write table, on every cell and for every collection whether it is ever aggregated or not, add a ninth index to every record write, and it made the selective filtered shape no faster (0.88 to 1.10; 0.79 in the loaded run). The [off-ramp](#off-ramp-to-a-dedicated-analytical-store) records when it would be justified; it would then land as a migration-only sub-slice, built concurrently as migration 050 is.

No maintained projection or partition is added either. A materialized aggregate projection (a rollup table kept by the jobs Worker) needs new tables, a maintainer and its own freshness and reconciliation, and nothing measured needs answers beyond the interactive ceiling sooner than a snapshot job gives them. Partitioning `record_index_values` by space would only shorten the whole-table scan above, the case the index addresses, at the cost of every writer, reader and the purge. What is added instead is the [freshness](#freshness) of the materialized results that exist, sealed snapshot job results, and the [reconciliation](#reconciliation) of the typed index projection every exact answer reads.

The script's write rounds measure it as they measure the candidate: 300 record creates through the real write path per round, alternately with and without the index, over a records table holding the fixture's 1,050,000 records. ## Mixed load

Two composed application services, as two isolates (`postgresServices`, each with a 2-connection interactive pool and a 1-connection analytical pool, origin limit 5, so one analytical slot for the cell), over the 500,000-record collection. Through the first isolate's interactive pool, a loop reads a record by ID and counts `score < 1000` together (one pair) for 10 s per phase, while: nothing else runs; the jobs Worker steps an export; six aggregation loops per isolate run `byValue`, each retrying a refusal after 5 ms; the same loops retrying after 250 ms; the 5 ms loops and the Worker together; the 250 ms loops and the Worker together. | Jobs Worker | 722 | 12.7 | 19.8 | 39.3 | — | 0 | 213 |

| Both, retry after 250 ms | 743 | 11.7 | 21.2 | 58.4 | 2 / 448 | 1 | 23 |

Each phase counts its outcomes over one interval, from its start to the end of its interactive reads: the read pairs and the aggregations answered or refused that completed in it, and the job result pages written in it by the database's clock (`analysis_result_pages.created_at`, between the interval's ends read on a connection held for the phase), counted once the phase has settled, so whichever step wrote a page and whenever it returns. A page written after the reads ended is counted only within `pageCutoffLagMs` of them, the time to read the interval's end (at most 8.1 ms in the recount run's phases running the Worker). The committed report and the loaded run counted a page only once its step returned, so their page counts omit the pages of the step still running when reads ended, up to one 2 s step: their 328, 15 and 168 (loaded run: 63 and none) are lower bounds, and the last column is the recount run's.

- No phase ran more aggregate statements at once than the cell's slots, and in the committed report every interactive read pair finished within 74 ms (loaded run: 405 ms; recount run, under host load rising to 30.8: 775 ms at most, p95 at most 280.7 ms).
- Most of what aggregations take from interactive reads is the refused attempts, not the statements: each refused attempt is still admitted on the isolate's interactive pool (directory read, scope and declaration check), since [STA-58](analysis-aggregations.md#analytical-capacity) authorizes every aggregation before it takes capacity. Twelve clients retrying every 5 ms raise the read pairs' median 2.4-fold (loaded run: 5.3-fold, 24.1 to 127.7 ms); retrying after 250 ms answers as many aggregations and keeps reads at their level alone (p50 9.6 and p95 12.5 ms against 11.2 and 13.5; loaded run: p50 35.9 against 24.1). So a `BACKPRESSURE` refusal is to be retried after at least 250 ms; STA-61's transports carry that as their retry hint.
- With one slot, aggregations and jobs share it. Retried every 5 ms, aggregations take it nearly each time it frees: the export wrote no page in that phase against 213 with the slot to itself (recount run). Retried after 250 ms, the export wrote 23 pages, 11% of its rate alone, under a host load that rose to 30.8; aggregations answered 2, and interactive read pairs' p95 was 21.2 ms, 1.6 times their 13.5 ms alone and near the 19.8 ms of the jobs Worker alone (the recount run's interactive figures are its host's load: p95 64.6 ms alone, 83.3 ms in this phase). So at an origin limit of 5 a cell's interactive aggregations take more than half its jobs' progress even when clients back off, and starve it when they do not; a cell with steady analytical demand needs a larger origin limit (4 slots at 20).

## Freshness

Every result says whether it is still the collection's:

| Result | `freshness` | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interactive aggregation (`analysis.aggregate`) | `{ state: 'exact', checkedAt }`, `checkedAt` equal to `snapshot.takenAt` | Always exact: the groups are the live records' own as one statement read them through the typed index, never a materialized copy. Per metric and dimension, `semantics` still says `exact` or `derived` (an average, a time bucket) |
| Snapshot job status, submission and page (`analysis.submit`, `status`, `page`) | `{ state: 'exact' \| 'stale', checkedAt, latestPosition, unpublished, schemaVersion }` | `exact` when, as of `checkedAt` (the reading statement's snapshot), no write to the collection was published past the job's watermark (`latestPosition`, the collection's last feed position, is at most the watermark), none awaits publication (`unpublished` is false) and the collection's schema version is the one pinned. Otherwise `stale`: the materialized result is still exact as of its watermark, and the writes after it are not in it |

The freshness is read in the same statement as the job's status, with the scope locks, grant check and final credential check every job read takes, so it never discloses more than the caller's capability on the collection allows: the collection's own last feed position and whether it has unpublished writes. A write committing after that statement is not seen; `checkedAt` says when it was taken. A stale result is never refreshed in place: submit a new job, whose watermark covers the writes. The export and aggregate pages themselves are immutable and carry their boundary (`watermark`, `records: 'as-of-watermark'`); freshness is reported beside them, never inside the sealed content.

A materialized result in this slice is a sealed snapshot job result: the measurements justify no maintained aggregate projection (see [typed indexes and projections](#typed-indexes-and-projections)), so there is no other copy to label.

## Reconciliation

`record_index_values` is a typed projection of each record's canonical data: record writers and backfill derive one row per live record and declared field (`missing` or `null` included), and every exact filter, sort and aggregation over a declared field reads it as the truth (an unfiltered count reads only the records). Readiness and migration 031's commit check guarantee a row exists; nothing checked until now that it holds what the data derives. A faulty writer, a partial restore or a manual repair could make an `exact` answer wrong without any error.

`reconcileIndexes(pool, spaceId, collectionId, { after?, maxRecords? })` (`packages/postgres/src/index-reconciliation.ts`) compares them:

- It pages through the collection's records (live and deleted) in record ID order, 1,000 at a time and at most 1 MiB of canonical data a page past its first record (the page bound of snapshot jobs, so a page of records near the 1 MiB record limit stays within the statement timeout), at most `maxRecords` (10,000 by default, 1 to 1,000,000) per call, and returns `next`, the last record checked, to continue from with `after`, set exactly when a record follows it in the page's snapshot (each page reads up to its record bound plus one record, 1,001 at most, before cutting at 1 MiB, so a page cut by bytes reads records past the ones it takes and the following pages read them again), so a bound ending at the collection's last record returns null.
- Each page is one `REPEATABLE READ, READ ONLY` transaction: the collection's current definition, its ready declarations, the records and their rows are one snapshot, so a write committing meanwhile is never drift. A declaration still backfilling is not checked; `fields` lists those checked on every page of the call (one turning ready during it is checked from then on, but not listed).
- Each live record's data is first validated whole, as the writers validate it before deriving (`validateParsedValue` of the complete record under the current definition). A record they accept is never underivable for a schema reason. A rejected record derives no row for the ready fields that explain the rejection (each validated against its own schema, a missing one only when required), or for every ready field when the rejection is the whole record's: a whole-record constraint such as a root `enum`, the rest of the record rejected, or no ready field explaining it. Definitions only add optional fields (existing fields and root constraints never change), so data a writer accepted stays accepted. Each accepted value is then derived as the writers and backfill derive it (`derivedIndexValue`) and compared with its row in the database's own types (`numeric`, `text COLLATE "C"`, `boolean`), so a change past a double's precision is still found.
- Drift is reported per record and field as `missing` (a live record without a row), `unexpected` (a deleted record still holding one), `kind`, `value`, or `underivable` (the writers would reject the stored value under the current definition, so it derives no row: a value of another type than its field's, outside its bounds, length, integer or enum constraint, a date-time field holding a non-instant, a field holding an object or array, a required field missing, a null on a non-nullable field, or a value past the index's byte limit or holding a character the database cannot store; and for every ready field when the rejection is the whole record's: a root `enum` the record is not a member of, an undeclared property, a missing required field that is not checked, or canonical data that is not a JSON object). An underivable field is never reported as `kind` or `value` drift, nor as clean when its row matches the rejected value, with the expected and actual kinds and never a value. Damaged data is drift of its record, never a failure of the call. `driftCount` counts all; at most 100 are listed.

It only reads and never repairs: drift on a ready field means exact answers over that collection may be wrong and is an incident to investigate. A live record's rows are rewritten by its next write. `pnpm db:reconcile-indexes --space ID --collection ID [--after RECORD_ID] [--max-records N]` runs it through `DATABASE_URL` (loopback, or `sslmode=verify-full`, as every operator script connects), prints the report as JSON. Without `--max-records` it continues call after call until the collection (after `--after`) is read to its end, as one report; with it, it reads at most that many records. It exits 0 when everything it was to read was read without drift, 2 when drift was found, 3 when the `--max-records` bound ended it before the collection's end without drift (`next` says where to continue with `--after`; that prefix only is checked), and 1 when the check itself failed (no report). It needs only `SELECT` on `records`, `record_index_values`, `collections`, `collection_versions` and `collection_index_declarations`, the tables every record read already reads. It authorizes no caller and reads every record of the collection, so it is an operator check, not a client operation. Measured on the fixture's 500,000-record collection with that validation at revision f11025e5 (byte-bounded pages, before a page counted its last record's bytes and read one record past its bound; not re-measured since): 43,304 to 45,406 records per second over four fields (three runs; host load 3.9 when the invocation started, before seeding, and 8.2 at its end, so not the load of the timed runs alone), so about 11 to 12 s per 500,000 records; the previous revision (per-field validation, record-only pages) measured 48,151 to 52,092 at load 3.4, and the full report's run before validation 51,491 (loaded run: 11,673, so up to 43 s).

## Off-ramp to a dedicated analytical store

Postgres stays the analytical store while the measured patterns fit their budgets. Measure the target with this script and the cell's own counters, and take the next step when any of these holds over a representative week:

1. **The typed index** above, as a migration-only sub-slice built concurrently: when interactive aggregations over a collection that holds a large share of its cell's index rows are refused with `time` in regular use, and the target measures the index's record write cost below 5%.
2. **A maintained aggregate projection in Postgres** (rollup tables kept from the event feed, with freshness and reconciliation as here; its migration first, as its own sub-slice): when the same aggregate spec over a collection past the [interactive ceiling](#limits) is requested again before a snapshot job over it would complete.
3. **A dedicated analytical store** (a columnar replica fed from the event feed, keeping as-of watermarks, authorization and freshness as here), when any of these holds:
   - collections that must be analysed whole exceed what one snapshot job covers within its budgets (here the 10,000,000-record bound, about 2.6 million records by step time in the loaded run, and about 544,000 records of this size by bytes for an export), and raising those budgets would hold analytical capacity past the jobs Worker's tick;
   - at the largest origin limit (4 analytical slots at 20), more than half of aggregation attempts are refused with `slots` over an hour, with clients retrying after 250 ms;
   - interactive reads' p95 under analytical load, with clients retrying after 250 ms and the jobs Worker running, exceeds twice their p95 without it on the target (here 21.2 ms against 13.5 ms alone, 1.6 times, in the phase running both; the loaded run did not measure that phase);
   - jobs make less than half their progress alone while aggregations retry after 250 ms at the largest origin limit. Not measured at that limit: the mixed load runs at an origin limit of 5, where it holds (23 pages against 213 in the recount run).

On the measured fixture the first three do not hold. The last holds at an origin limit of 5, the only one measured, and is not measured at the largest origin limit it names; measuring it there (the target's origin limit, in external acceptance) decides it. Until then Postgres stays the analytical store, and a cell with steady analytical demand runs at the larger origin limit, as [mixed load](#mixed-load) says.
