# Retrieval projection storage (STA-44)

Migration `045_retrieval_projections.sql` adds the storage for rebuildable lexical and vector projections and makes authoritative evidence writes enqueue projection work. It is the first of six slices of STA-14: the embedding port and indexing worker (STA-45, [below](#indexing-worker-sta-45)), hybrid search (STA-46), context bundles (STA-47), generations, rebuild and model migration (STA-48) and transport, evaluation and docs (STA-49) follow. There is no search, bundle, HTTP, CLI or MCP surface yet, and nothing starts or moves generations except SQL (STA-48 adds that).

Projections are derived, rebuildable data. Records, sources and claims stay independently readable without them. Every projection row names the canonical Ref (space, collection, kind, ID), authoritative revision and generation it was built from, so a later search can reread and reauthorize each hit at authority and reject stale or deleted ones. No projection row is evidence that its Ref is current, readable or complete.

## Tables

| Table | Contents | Mutability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `retrieval_policies` | Per-collection policy versions: record fields (JSON Pointers), source extraction content, claim statements, lexical and vector switches, built-in text search configuration, allowed embedding processing regions | Immutable; versions start at 1 without gaps and are published in order, one successor of the current version at a time (even within one multirow `INSERT`; otherwise `retrieval_policies_order`, `23514`); the highest is current |
| `retrieval_collection_heads` | Per collection with a policy: its current policy version and the highest generation number it has allocated | Written only by owner-rights triggers, under an update lock, when a policy is published or a generation starts, so its policy version only advances and is always the highest; kept until erasure |
| `vector_index_strategies` | ANN strategy per accepted embedding dimension, with its evidence | Cell reference data, seeded by the migration |
| `projection_generations` | A collection's projection build under one policy version and, for vectors, one embedder, model, model version and dimensions | `building` → `verifying` → `active` → `retired`; `building` or `verifying` may be abandoned to `retired`. Each transition stamps its own time. Kept until erasure |
| `projection_refs` | Per generation and Ref: the authoritative revision (and source extraction version) its chunks were built from (for a Ref projected for the first time, are being built from), or that it was deleted | Moves forward only; a deleted Ref stays deleted; kept until erasure |
| `lexical_chunks` | A code-point range of one configured text, its content and a `tsvector` (GIN index) | Immutable; replaced by delete and insert |
| `vector_chunks` | The same Ref, revision, generation, offsets and policy, plus embedder metadata, processing region, digest of the embedded text and the embedding | Immutable; replaced by delete and insert |
| `embedding_checkpoints` | Embeddings computed outside SQL and saved before they are applied, keyed by the Ref's target revision and chunk; references the Ref's live `projection_refs` row in the generation | Immutable; removed when applied or superseded, and before the Ref is deleted |
| `projection_deletion_receipts` | A Ref removed from one generation: revision, chunks removed, time; references the Ref's deleted `projection_refs` row | Immutable; kept until erasure |
| `retrieval_outbox` | One unit of projection work per source extraction, claim revision and source or claim deletion | Delivery fields only; delivery is final; kept until erasure |

Every space-scoped table carries `space_id` and its owning `collection_id`; every key path ends at `collections`. `vector_index_strategies` is cell configuration and holds no space data.

## Chunk invariants

Each chunk row carries:

- **Canonical Ref**: `space_id`, `collection_id`, `ref_kind` (`record`, `source`, `claim`), `ref_id`.
- **Authoritative revision**: `revision` (the record or `entity_refs` revision) and, for a source, `extraction_version`. A chunk references its `projection_refs` row at exactly that revision and extraction while the Ref is live, so a chunk can never sit beside a newer revision or a deletion: advancing or deleting a Ref fails while older chunks remain. `projection_refs` names an existing record or `entity_refs` row (checked at COMMIT, like `projection_heads`, so applying takes no key-share lock on authority rows). Deleting a record or identity checks those foreign keys from the referencing side, once per deleted row, so `projection_refs` has partial indexes on the referencing columns (`projection_refs_record` on `(space_id, collection_id, record_id)`, `projection_refs_entity` on `(space_id, collection_id, entity_kind, entity_id)`); without them each check scans the table.
- **Generation and policy**: `generation` and `policy_version`, which must be the generation's own policy version. A generation is built under exactly one policy version, the collection's current one when it starts, so a policy change (fields, content or regions) takes a new generation. Publishing a policy and starting a generation both write the collection's `retrieval_collection_heads` row, and a generation reads its current policy and the last generation number from that row under an update lock before advancing it. So a generation whose transaction started before a newer policy or a higher generation committed is refused: under `READ COMMITTED` it waits for the other writer and then fails `projection_generations_policy` or `projection_generations_order` (`23514`); under `REPEATABLE READ` it fails with `40001`. A lower number can never start after a higher one, even once the higher one is retired.
- **Offsets**: `chunk_index`, `content_start` and `content_end` (code points in the projected text: the record field's value, the extraction content or the claim statement), and for a source optional `source_start` and `source_end` in the original. A lexical chunk's `content` is exactly its range. A record chunk names its `field`.
- **Embedder metadata (vector only)**: `embedder`, `embedding_model`, `model_version` and `dimensions`, which must equal the generation's, the region the embedding was processed in, which both the generation's policy and the collection's current policy must allow (so narrowing a policy's regions stops embedding in a removed region for every generation still being written, including the active one built under an older policy), and `content_sha256` of the embedded text. The embedding has exactly `dimensions` dimensions.

An insert trigger also refuses content the policy does not configure (an unlisted record field, source content or claim statements when switched off), a lexical chunk when lexical projection is off, a vector chunk or checkpoint when vectors are off, and any write into a retired generation. A vector chunk or checkpoint reads the current policy from the collection's head row under a share lock, so publishing a policy (or starting a generation) waits for embedders already writing, and an embedder that meets a newer policy is checked against it under `READ COMMITTED` or fails with `40001` under `REPEATABLE READ`; a `REPEATABLE READ` embedder whose snapshot predates any policy or generation start then retries. It computes `tsv` from `content` with the policy's built-in text search configuration, whatever the writer sends. Writes share-lock their generation row, so retiring a generation waits for writers already in it (they commit into it before it retires), and a writer that reaches the row while a retirement is in flight waits and is then refused: `23514` (`projection_generations_retired`) under `READ COMMITTED`, `40001` under `REPEATABLE READ`.

A Ref moves forward by revision, then deletion, then extraction version: deleting it at its current revision is forward whether the deleted row keeps or clears `extraction_version`, while a live row never moves to an older extraction and a deleted row never changes.

A deletion receipt needs the authoritative Ref deleted (a tombstoned record or `entity_refs` row) and, by foreign key, the Ref's `projection_refs` row in that generation deleted at the receipt's revision. A worker therefore marks the projected Ref deleted (an update of its live row, or an insert of a deleted row for a Ref the generation never projected) before recording the receipt. That row is the only fence between a receipt and an apply of the same Ref, and it holds under every isolation level: both write the same row or key, a deleted row never changes again and its key admits no second row. An apply whose snapshot predates a committed deletion fails with a serialization error (`40001`) under `REPEATABLE READ`, with the order check under `READ COMMITTED`, or with a unique violation (`23505`) if it inserts; a deletion whose snapshot predates a committed apply fails the same way or, under `READ COMMITTED`, deletes the newer revision after it. Once recorded, the Ref cannot be projected live in that generation again, including by a rebuild into a generation that has the receipt.

The same row fences embedding checkpoints, so no embedding of deleted text sits beside a receipt. A checkpoint references the Ref's live `projection_refs` row in its generation, at whatever revision that row is (a checkpoint is keyed by the Ref's target revision, which may be ahead of it). A worker therefore records the Ref in the generation before saving checkpoints: a Ref projected for the first time gets its live row at its target revision, with its chunks to follow, and advancing a Ref with checkpoints is allowed. Deleting the Ref is refused (`23503`) while any checkpoint of it in that generation remains, whatever the checkpoint's revision, so a worker removes the Ref's checkpoints with its chunks before deleting it, and the receipt follows only after that. A checkpoint is refused (`23503`) for a Ref the generation has not recorded or has deleted. Under concurrency: a checkpoint whose snapshot predates a committed deletion fails with `40001` under `REPEATABLE READ` and `23503` under `READ COMMITTED`; a deletion whose snapshot predates a committed checkpoint fails with `23503` at either level, because the deletion's check sees committed checkpoints; and a Ref recorded live and deleted by two transactions conflicts on its key (`23505`). A receipt covers one generation's projection rows only. It is not proof of erasure of authority rows, original bytes, other generations, WAL or backups.

## Projection work

Authoritative writes enqueue work in their own transaction, by owner-rights triggers, so no write path can skip it and a writer needs no outbox privilege:

| Authoritative write | Work |
| --- | --- |
| A new extraction version (STA-34 extract) | `source` `upsert` at the source's revision and the new extraction version |
| A claim revision: create (revision 1), revise and review (each a new revision) | `claim` `upsert` at that revision |
| A source or claim deletion (its `entity_refs` tombstone) | `delete` at the Ref's revision |

The work row records the space's placement generation at commit, as `projection_outbox` and `evidence_outbox` do. A rolled-back or refused write (for example a claim whose key loses its grants before COMMIT, migration 043) leaves no work; a replayed write inserts nothing and enqueues nothing. If the work cannot be enqueued, the authoritative write fails. Record writes already enqueue `projection_outbox` work in their transaction (migration 002), so records need no second queue. Deleting a source or claim also enqueues an `evidence_outbox` invalidation (migration 044); the retrieval queue is separate so its delivery state can be owned by the projection worker. The [indexing worker](#indexing-worker-sta-45) consumes `retrieval_outbox`; pending work is erased with its space.

Migration 045 enqueues, for evidence written before it, each live source's latest extraction and each live claim's current revision. Deleted evidence has nothing to project, and neither has a space being erased (`deleting`, or `deleted` awaiting an erasure repair). That also keeps an erasure already running migration 044's purge routine safe across the upgrade: it can wait on 045's locks and then resume with the old routine, which removes no retrieval work, so no work may reference its space's identities; it completes after 045 commits. A space whose erasure starts during the upgrade cannot reach that routine: before the backfill, 045 share locks every space it enqueues for, so an update of such a space's row (a lifecycle change such as `deleting`, or a policy, key or placement change) waits until 045 commits, and its purge then runs 045's routine; a space that commits `deleting` while that lock waits is rechecked and skipped. Those updates touch no table 045 locks, and a purge holds its space row only once the space is committed `deleting`, which 045 skips without locking, so this wait cannot deadlock; it is bounded by the migrator's lock wait like 045's table locks.

## ANN index strategy

| Dimensions | Strategy | Parameters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 384, 768, 1024, 1536 | HNSW partial expression index `((embedding::vector(N)) vector_cosine_ops) WHERE dimensions = N` | `m = 16`, `ef_construction = 64` |
| 3072 | Exact distance within a generation's filters, no ANN index | — |

Evidence and reasoning:

- One `vector` column holds every dimension, so each recorded dimension has its own partial expression index; [pgvector's FAQ documents this pattern](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector) for mixed dimensions. A generation must name a recorded dimension (foreign key), so an embedder with an unrecorded dimension needs a migration that records its strategy first. A query uses the index only with the same cast and `dimensions` predicate.
- HNSW rather than IVFFlat: IVFFlat derives its lists from the rows present when the index is built, and this migration builds on an empty table; HNSW needs no training step and accepts inserts incrementally. `m = 16` and `ef_construction = 64` are pgvector's defaults, not tuned values; STA-49's relevance evaluation measures recall and latency and may record different parameters.
- pgvector 0.8 refuses HNSW and IVFFlat indexes on `vector` columns above 2,000 dimensions (observed: `column cannot have more than 2000 dimensions for hnsw index`). 3,072 dimensions is therefore exact. Indexing it would need `halfvec` or reduced dimensions, a later decision.
- Cosine distance only, matching the operator class. The integration test confirms on PostgreSQL 17 with pgvector 0.8.7 that a 384-dimension nearest-neighbour query with the index's cast and predicate plans an index scan on `vector_chunks_hnsw_384`, and that, narrowed to one space, collection and generation, the planner may instead read that generation through the primary key and rank it exactly. A filtered ANN top-k is not complete recall: STA-46 must overfetch, report underfill and never treat either plan's result as complete.

These are storage choices on small local fixtures, not capacity, latency or recall measurements.

## Bounds

Initial storage bounds, chosen for bounded rows and validation, not measured provider limits: up to 32 record fields per policy, each a JSON Pointer of at most 256 bytes; up to 16 processing regions, each a lowercase label of at most 63 characters; lexical chunk content of at most 65,536 bytes (well inside PostgreSQL's 1 MB `tsvector` limit); embedder name up to 64 characters and model and model version up to 256 bytes; work errors up to 1,024 bytes. Chunk size, overlap, embedding retry and worker budgets are STA-45's [initial bounds](#bounds-of-the-worker).

## Upgrade, restore and grants

Migration 045 takes share row exclusive locks on `collections`, `records`, `claim_revisions`, `entity_refs` and `extraction_versions` up front, within the migrator's five-second bounded wait, and holds them until it commits. A writer holding them past the wait makes it fail with `Retrieval projection upgrade waits for record, extraction and claim writers; retry when long evidence transactions finish` and roll back completely (no table, trigger, ledger row or purge change); a retry applies it. Claim revise and review insert a revision before they advance the claim's `entity_refs` row, the order 045 locks in, so the step simply waits for them. A claim create writes its `entity_refs` row first; if one interleaves with the step, PostgreSQL resolves the cycle by aborting one side as a deadlock (`40P01`). When that is the migration, the migrator reports the same retry message; when it is the writer, that one request fails with the deadlock and nothing of it is written.

While the step holds its locks, record, source, extraction and claim writes wait for it. Apart from creating empty tables and indexes, its time is the enqueue backfill, which writes one `retrieval_outbox` row per live source with an extraction and per live claim. It reads every source and claim identity (`entity_refs`, live or deleted) and, per live identity, one primary-key probe for the source's latest extraction or the claim's current revision; it never reads older extractions or revisions, so their number does not change its time. Before running 045's SQL, the migrator takes the same locks in the same order and counts `entity_refs` rows up to 10,001. Above 10,000 identities it refuses with `Populated retrieval projection upgrade requires drained traffic above 10000 source and claim identities` and applies nothing, unless `STATEPLANE_POPULATED_INDEX_UPGRADE=drained` is set; set it only after stopping record, source and claim writers. Deleted identities count toward the bound because the backfill reads them.

Measured on local PostgreSQL 17 with pgvector 0.8.7 in Docker on an 8-core Apple Silicon host under load (load average 14 to 21), three runs each of the whole step in one transaction, rolled back (the step alone on an empty database takes 41 to 45 ms):

| Identities (half sources, half claims) | Live | Versions or revisions each | History rows | Enqueued | Step time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | all | 20 | 200,000 | 10,000 | 279 to 615 ms |
| 10,000 | all | 100 | 1,000,000 | 10,000 | 341 to 456 ms |
| 10,000 | 10% | 100 | 1,000,000 | 1,000 | 61 to 84 ms |
| 100,000 | all | 2 | 200,000 | 100,000 | 3.4 to 3.7 s |

At the bound the writer stall is therefore at most about two thirds of a second, inside source and claim writers' two-second lock wait, after which they answer `BACKPRESSURE`; above it, it grows by about 35 microseconds per identity. The plan is a scan of `entity_refs` with a backward index-only scan of `extraction_versions_pkey` (or a `claim_revisions_pkey` probe) per live identity. These are local measurements, not Railway figures; measure the backfill on the target before relying on the bound there. The migrator's decision holds through commit, because the locks do. As for every migration in this repository (see [evidence storage](evidence-storage.md)), 045 is forward-only and "down" means transactional rollback, not a reverse script: the step is one transaction, and a step that fails or is refused leaves no table, trigger, ledger row or purge change and can be retried (`migration 045 rolls back atomically …` in `test/integration/retrieval-projections.test.js`). Recovery from a bad deploy is restore.

`stateplane_purge_space` now removes every space-scoped retrieval row before the records, identities and collections it references, and the space erasure report (`PostgresSpaces.erasure`) reads every space-scoped retrieval table (all but `vector_index_strategies`, which holds no space data) before reporting the database erased. The purge deletes `projection_refs` before the records and identities, in the same transaction, so at COMMIT each deleted record or identity runs one deferred foreign-key check against `projection_refs`; the referencing-side indexes keep each check an index probe. Measured locally (PostgreSQL 17 + pgvector 0.8.7, loaded 8-core host): a space with 4,000 records and 4,000 claims projected in three generations (24,000 `projection_refs` rows) runs its 8,000 deferred checks in 0.11–0.13 s with the indexes and 2.1–2.2 s without them (two runs each; the rest of the purge took 1.6–1.9 s either way). Without the indexes each check is a sequential scan of the table, so the time grows with records × projected Refs: at 120,000 `projection_refs` rows in one collection a reviewer measured 36–50 ms per check.

Migration 046 adds `retrieval_dispatch_checkpoint` and a nullable `last_error_at` on `projection_outbox` and `retrieval_outbox` (with `retrieval_outbox`'s update guard allowing it as a delivery column); it runs under the migrator's five-second lock wait (adding each column waits for record writers, outbox workers and space purges already running, then takes a moment). It locks `projection_outbox` before `retrieval_outbox`, the order space purges and status reads use, so a purge in flight finishes and the step then applies; a writer holding them the other way round can still make PostgreSQL abort the step as a deadlock, and that, like a lock wait past five seconds, rolls the step back and fails with retry guidance. The migrator commits 045 before 046, so a purge started while 045 applies finishes rather than deadlocking with 046's change to the record outbox. Migration 047 builds the `retrieval_outbox_age` index over pending evidence work with `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY`, on its own outside any transaction (still under the migrator's session lock), so evidence writers, retrieval workers and purges keep running while it scans a populated outbox; it waits for transactions already writing the outbox and for older snapshots, without blocking them. An interrupted build leaves an invalid index and 047 unrecorded; the next run drops it concurrently, builds it again and records 047 only once the index is valid. Status works without the index meanwhile, reading the backlog instead of one row. 045 is unchanged, so a cell that applied it upgrades without checksum drift.

Run `pnpm db:grant-retrieval` after migration 046 and after every restore. It grants the cell operational role `SELECT` on every retrieval table (space deletion reads the space-scoped ones to prove erasure); `INSERT` on `retrieval_policies` (its trigger maintains `retrieval_collection_heads`, which the role cannot write); `INSERT, UPDATE` on `projection_generations` and `projection_refs`; `INSERT` on `projection_deletion_receipts`; `INSERT, DELETE` on `lexical_chunks`, `vector_chunks` and `embedding_checkpoints`; `UPDATE` on `retrieval_outbox`; `SELECT, UPDATE` on `retrieval_dispatch_checkpoint`; and `EXECUTE` on `public.vector(public.vector, integer, boolean)`. The last is needed because an HNSW index casts each inserted embedding to its dimension and PostgreSQL checks `EXECUTE` on that cast function for the inserting role; the [regional runbook](regional-topology.md) revokes `PUBLIC` execution of public-schema routines, and without the grant a vector insert fails with `permission denied for function vector`. No grant allows `DELETE` or `TRUNCATE` of policies, generations, projected Refs, receipts or work: removal stays with the scoped purge routine. Policy validation runs in triggers, so writers need no other routine grant.

## Indexing worker (STA-45)

The jobs Worker chunks, embeds and projects configured content into every generation of a collection that is not retired (`building`, `verifying` and `active`). Projections stay derived data: exact record, source and claim reads and writes never call the embedder or wait for projection work, and nothing here returns projection content to a caller.

### Embedding port and registry

`@stateplane/retrieval` defines the port. An `Embedder` has a profile (embedder, model, model version, dimensions, processing region, relative quality, relative cost, `maxBatch` and `maxInputCodePoints`) and `embed(texts, signal)`, an external call that returns one vector per input. A provider that cannot be reached, is overloaded or gives up on its signal throws `EmbedderUnavailableError` (code `EMBEDDER_UNAVAILABLE`); anything else it throws, and an answer that is not one finite, nonzero vector of its dimensions per input (`EmbeddingRejectedError`), is the input's. `EmbeddingRegistry` holds the configured embedders, one per identity and region, each as a frozen snapshot of its validated profile with its `embed`, and returns those snapshots, so changing a profile after registration changes no selection, region check, limit or recorded identity. Before every call the snapshot's `embed` rereads the adapter's own profile and, unless it can be read and still matches the snapshot in every field (a mutated or replaced profile, or a profile, field getter or proxy that throws, does not), refuses with a rejected `EmbedderUnavailableError` without calling the adapter, which the worker settles as an outage, uncounted, so an adapter never runs under an identity, region or limit other than the one authorized:

- `select({ regions, dimensions, inputCodePoints, minQuality })` chooses an embedder for a new generation (STA-48 starts generations): only in a region the policy allows, with a dimension the cell has an index strategy for, accepting the chunker's input and of at least the required quality; then the lowest cost, the highest quality, the earliest allowed region and the name.
- `resolve(identity, regions, inputCodePoints)` returns the embedder with exactly a generation's identity in the first allowed region that has one. The worker uses only this, so a generation's vectors always come from its own model and version.

`DeterministicEmbedder` is a local test embedder: signed feature hashing of lowercased word tokens, L2-normalized (texts sharing words are closer). It calls nothing external and is not a semantic model.

The jobs Worker's registry is empty: no provider's processing region has been qualified for a cell (a regional label is not proof of residency). Generations without vectors therefore apply, and each vector generation keeps its previous state, its work stays due and status reports `degraded` with `embedderAvailable: false`, until a qualified provider is configured ([below](#prepare-and-apply)).

### Chunker

Only configured content is chunked, indexed or embedded: a record's policy-listed fields (JSON Pointers, in policy order) whose values are strings, a source's latest extraction content when `source_content` is on, and a claim revision's statement when `claim_statements` is on. Other fields, values of other types, unlisted kinds and everything else about a Ref are never read into a chunk. A text is split by code points into chunks of at most 1,000 code points, cutting after the last whitespace in a chunk's second half when there is one, with 100 code points of overlap; whitespace-only chunks are dropped. A record string may hold U+0000 or an unpaired surrogate, which PostgreSQL text cannot store; chunk content holds U+FFFD in its place, one code point for one, so offsets stay those of the authoritative text and such a record projects instead of failing. `chunk_index` numbers a Ref's chunks across its fields, so lexical and vector chunks of one revision share indexes and offsets. The vector side stores the SHA-256 of each chunk's text (`content_sha256`).

### Prepare and apply

Each delivery projects one Ref's authoritative target: a revision (with, for a source, its extraction version) and its content, or its deletion.

Every retrieval write transaction (preparation's plan and checkpoint saves, an evidence item's apply, and a record's fallback apply) first share locks the Ref's space and collection rows through its commit and proceeds only while the space is `active` or `readOnly`, the collection is not deleted and the space is still at the placement generation the work runs under (a record delivery's, or the one an evidence item read when it started), as completion's scope check does. Suspension, deletion, erasure and a placement fence update those rows, so each either commits before such a transaction, which then sees it and writes nothing, or waits for its commit; erasure locks the space first, so its purge never meets a checkpoint or Ref written after it began.

1. **Prepare**, outside any SQL transaction. In short transactions of its own it reads the collection's generations and, for each vector generation, records the Ref in the generation at its target revision when the generation has never projected it (so checkpoints can reference it), drops checkpoints whose region the policies no longer allow, and finds the chunks that still need an embedding. Embeddings of the same text already in the generation, from an earlier revision's vector chunks or checkpoints, are reused, so a claim review (a new revision with the same statement) calls no embedder. The rest are embedded in batches of the embedder's `maxBatch`, each batch saved as `embedding_checkpoints` in its own transaction; no connection is held while the embedder runs. A generation that has deleted the Ref, holds a newer revision or already holds this one completely is skipped. Content is sent only to an embedder whose region both the generation's policy and the collection's current policy allow; when none is configured, that generation's preparation fails with `EMBEDDER_UNAVAILABLE` before any call. Before every embedding call, each retry included, a short transaction rechecks that the space and collection are open, the generation is not retired and both policies still allow the embedder's region; a suspension, deletion or narrower policy committed since stops that generation's preparation (`PROJECTION_INCOMPLETE`) before more content leaves. A call already in flight when a narrower policy commits still completes, and the storage trigger then refuses its checkpoints; that refusal is reported the same way, so the work runs again at once under the new policy rather than failing. A checkpoint is saved only while the scope is open, the generation's Ref is live and not past the target (share locked, so an apply waits or is seen) and the target is not yet complete there, so a late preparation leaves nothing behind. A generation that cannot be prepared does not stop the others: preparation tries every vector generation and then reports the first error that is the target's own, else the first outage. Each vector generation with work gets an equal share of the preparation budget's remaining time (the last one all of it), so an embedder that fails, hangs or ignores its signal cannot spend another generation's time. Each generation's cut is judged by its own progress, whatever its siblings saved: one cut by its share before saving anything counts as an outage (the work waits with outage backoff instead of rerunning at once), and later items in the same evidence step skip that embedder (as they skip one that failed) without calling it. A healthy embedder whose single call outlasts its share is treated the same way while another vector generation has work: the shares are initial protective bounds, not measured provider latency.
2. **Apply**, inside the delivery's fenced transaction (with a 1-second lock wait), for each generation that is not retired, after share locking it so retirement waits: a generation that has deleted the Ref or holds a newer revision (or extraction) is left alone. A deletion removes the Ref's lexical and vector chunks and checkpoints, marks its `projection_refs` row deleted at the deletion's revision (inserting a deleted row for a Ref the generation never projected, which keeps a later stale delivery out) and records a deletion receipt. A live target replaces the Ref's chunks with its configured chunks: lexical chunks when the policy enables them, vector chunks only from checkpoints of exactly that revision, chunk and text digest in an allowed region. A vector generation missing any of them (an outage, no configured embedder, interrupted preparation) keeps its chunks, lexical side included, so a Ref in a generation is always at one revision; the other generations apply, and the work stays due for it (below). A policy narrowing that commits between the region read and the insert is refused by the storage trigger and reported as `PROJECTION_INCOMPLETE`: the apply rolls back and runs again, uncounted. Applied checkpoints, and older ones, are then removed; a generation already at or past the target only has its checkpoints up to its own position removed, so a superseded or late preparation leaves none behind once any later delivery of the Ref applies. Every applied position leaves the Ref's `projection_refs` row at it, with no chunks when the generation's policy configures no content the target has (a record without its configured fields, a whitespace-only extraction), so a preparation of an older position that starts later finds the generation past it and neither records the Ref nor saves a checkpoint. A Ref's first row is inserted only if absent and then locked: concurrent first applies of one Ref (overlapping ticks, or a record's fallback apply and completion) serialize on it, and the later one compares forward like any other instead of failing on the key. A generation whose policy configures nothing for the Ref's kind is not touched.
3. **Authority fence**, last in the same transaction: the Ref's authoritative rows are locked against their writers and reread. A record's row is share locked; a claim's `entity_refs` row is share locked (its revisions and deletion update it); a source's extraction lock is taken shared and its `entity_refs` row share locked (extractions and the source's deletion hold that lock exclusively). If authority no longer holds the applied target, the transaction rolls back and the work runs again with fresh state, so a revision, extraction or deletion that commits between the item's reread and its commit is never projected over. When no live generation configures the Ref's kind, apply writes nothing and the fence is skipped, so its writers never wait for it. Taken just before commit, the locks make a writer wait only for that commit, never for an apply or an embedder; a writer already holding its lock makes the fence wait at most the 1-second lock wait, after which the work is released as an outage.

Ordering and duplicates therefore cannot activate stale content in any generation: a projected Ref only moves forward, a deleted one stays deleted, and the storage's foreign keys and order check (above) refuse anything else even if a caller bypassed the worker.

**Records** use the record outbox ([durable outbox](durable-outbox.md)): its queue consumer calls `outbox.process(hint, new RecordRetrievalProjector(pool))`. The outbox already calls `apply` only for the record's latest committed revision, moves the per-ref head forward only and fences lease, scope and placement; prepare runs between its start and completion transactions. When some generation cannot be prepared, the generations that do not need it apply at once in a transaction of the projector's own, under completion's fences held through its commit (the scope fence above with the delivery's placement, and the authority fence) and within at most 3 s and the delivery's own budget, and the error is then thrown. The projector bounds itself by the delivery's deadline, which the outbox passes to `prepare`: preparation gets at most half the time the delivery has left and its grace at most an eighth of that, the fallback apply ends at least 250 ms (at most an eighth of the time left) before the delivery's deadline, and a backstop at that point ends anything still running as an outage, so even a short `processingMs` never turns an embedder outage into the delivery's counted failure (when the scope has closed, `prepare` returns and completion releases the delivery): an outage or interrupted preparation releases the lease without spending the failure budget and records `last_error`; any other error counts toward the dead letter, as for every projector. The delivery therefore stays due, and the record's projection head does not move, until every generation has the revision; later attempts find the applied generations complete and leave them alone. A record changed after completion read it fails the fence with `PROJECTION_INCOMPLETE` and is released uncounted; the outbox then supersedes it.

**Sources and claims** use `retrieval_outbox`, through `PostgresRetrievalWorker.step`. A claim leases due items by moving `available_at` 60 seconds ahead; that exact timestamp is the lease. Starting an item increments its `attempts` and renews the lease, so `attempts` counts started attempts only and backoff grows with them (a start whose transaction rolls back spends nothing and is released under the claim's lease with its error recorded); only the current lease, while it lasts, can start, complete or release an item. Items a step has no time left to start (it starts one only with at least 1 s of its budget left) are made due again at once, with no attempt spent, the cursor below moves back to the last pair whose item started (or where the claim found it, unless another claim has moved it since), so pairs whose claimed items never started keep their turn, and the step ends. Space/collection pairs with pending work take turns as a ring: each claim walks up to 100 of them, starting after the last pair a claim served (a cell-wide cursor, the single row of `retrieval_dispatch_checkpoint`, migration 046, kept across steps, ticks and workers and naming only a position) and wrapping around, skipping spaces that are not `active` or `readOnly` and deleted collections (their work stays pending, unclaimed and uncounted), and takes up to 2 due items from each and 8 in all, serving them in ring order. A due pair is therefore served within as many claims as there are due pairs ahead of it, however long their backlogs. A step ends when a claim's walk covered the whole ring, or 1,000 walked pairs, without finding anything due. Each item reads its Ref's **current** authoritative state fresh (the `entity_refs` revision and tombstone, the source's latest extraction or the claim's current revision), prepares it, and then applies in one transaction, within the item's budget, that takes the scope fence, locks the item's lease and rereads the authoritative state. If the space closed, its placement generation changed (a placement fence) or the Ref changed since it was read, or the authority fence finds it changed before commit, the item is released at once, uncounted, to run again with fresh state; its next attempt runs under the new placement, as the record outbox requeues a latest revision under it (the generation recorded in the work row is the one at enqueue, kept for provenance, since every item projects current authority); otherwise the target is applied. With every generation applied the item becomes `delivered`; with a vector generation left behind, the others' apply commits and the item stays pending, in the same transaction, with the backoff and `last_error` of what stopped that generation. Because every item projects the Ref's current state, a duplicate, late or reordered item applies nothing older. An expired lease cannot complete (`lost`).

### Failures, outages and status

| Situation | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Embedder unreachable, overloaded or not configured in an allowed region | Each batch is tried three times (waiting 250 ms, then 1 s, within the budget). Generations that do not need that embedder apply; the item is then released with backoff (from 5 s, doubling, at most 5 minutes) and `last_error` `EmbedderUnavailableError EMBEDDER_UNAVAILABLE`. Later items in the same step skip that embedder. Record work is released uncounted by the outbox. |
| Preparation runs out of its budget | It stops starting batches after 15 s (for a record, after half the delivery's time left; for an evidence item, after half the item's budget, which is at most 20 s and less at the end of a step), gets up to 1 s (at most a quarter of the item's budget) to save the batch it has, and the item is released with no error and due at once if it saved checkpoints (`PROJECTION_INCOMPLETE`; the next attempt continues from them), or as an outage if it saved none. Preparation and its grace therefore end before the item's own budget; if the caller's budget ends first anyway, the outcome is the same, never the item's failure. |
| Processing policy narrowed during preparation or apply | That generation stops before any further call to the removed region; the work runs again at once with no error, under the new policy (`PROJECTION_INCOMPLETE`). |
| Authority changed before commit | Rolled back and released due at once, with no error. |
| Space suspended or being deleted, or collection deleted, during preparation | No further embedding call and no checkpoint; the item is released with no error and waits unclaimed until the space reopens. A record delivery is released by its completion. |
| Placement fence during preparation | The same: no further call and no checkpoint. An evidence item is released uncounted and its next attempt runs under the new placement; a record delivery is released by its completion and requeued by the fence's reconciliation. |
| A transaction still running when its budget ends | Every worker transaction is bounded: preparation's (generation reads, plans, admission checks and checkpoint saves) by its budget and grace, ending at once when the caller's budget does, so a batch received after the grace is never saved; an item's start and apply by the item's budget; a claim by the step's; and settling or releasing what the step holds by 1 s, ending at most 1 s after the step's budget. A bounded transaction checks its session out within its budget (a session that arrives later goes back unused, and BEGIN is not sent), sends no statement, from BEGIN to COMMIT or ROLLBACK, once its deadline passes or its signal aborts, and bounds each of the body's statements by the time it has left (`statement_timeout`, lowered whenever the time left falls more than 100 ms below it). At the deadline or an abort it stops waiting for the statement in flight (BEGIN, the setup, COMMIT and ROLLBACK included), cancels it from another session (only while that backend is still in the same transaction; the cancellation, checkout included, gives up within 1 s and discards its own session if unanswered, so on a one-session pool it holds the session no longer) and discards its session, so its locks go at once and a one-session pool is free again; a COMMIT cut short that way has an unknown outcome. An apply cut short is released with outage backoff and `last_error` `DeadlineExceededError`; a start cut short is released under the claim's lease the same way, with no attempt spent; a claim cut short ends the step, and leases a cut-short claim's COMMIT may have taken expire unstarted. A record's fallback apply whose 3 s or delivery budget ends gives way the same way, commits nothing, and the delivery's own outage is reported. |
| Lock wait, serialization failure, lost session | Released with outage backoff and its code as `last_error`. |
| Any other error, such as an embedder rejecting an input or a statement timeout | Retried with backoff from 5 s doubling to one hour and its bounded diagnostic in `last_error` (name and code only for coded errors, as for the record outbox; at most 1,024 UTF-8 bytes, cut between characters). Nothing an embedder throws is stored: a provider message can quote its input, so the port replaces every error from `embed` with `EmbedderUnavailableError` (an outage: `EmbedderUnavailableError` itself or a transient fault such as `ECONNRESET`) or `EmbedderFailedError` (anything else, counted as the input's), each with a fixed message and no cause. `retrieval_outbox` has no dead letter, so the item stays pending and reported as degraded. Generations that do not need what failed still apply. |
| Unknown COMMIT outcome | The lease is kept and expires; the item is then claimed again. |

`PostgresRetrievalWorker.status(spaceId, collectionId)` reports a collection's projection state: `degraded` when sampled record or evidence work has an error (including an uncounted outage release) or a vector generation has no configured embedder in a region its policies allow, `pending` when work is waiting, otherwise `current`; per queue, pending and degraded counts over at most the 500 oldest-due rows (for records also at most 500 failed rows; `capped` marks lower bounds) with the most recently recorded error among them (by `last_error_at`, migration 046, which every write of `last_error` sets and every clear empties, so a lease, a backoff or parking never changes which error is newest; an error recorded before 046 has no time and ranks oldest; ties go to the later event or job; for records the newer of the two samples' newest errors; failed rows are sampled in event order, so the same rows on every call); the oldest pending evidence item over all of the collection's pending items (one row read through the pending enqueue-time index `retrieval_outbox_age`, migration 047; PostgreSQL picks that index from the table's statistics, so they must be current: autovacuum's analyze keeps them so, and a large burst of enqueues can be followed by `ANALYZE retrieval_outbox`, until which the lookup may sort the collection's pending rows instead), one backed off behind newer work included; and each generation's state, policy, projections, embedder identity and `embedderAvailable`. It is an operator and service read; transports come with STA-49.

### Bounds of the worker

Initial protective values, chosen for bounded statements and leases, not measured provider capacity or relevance (STA-49 evaluates chunking): chunks of 1,000 code points with 100 of overlap (at most 4,000 bytes, inside the 65,536-byte storage bound; an embedder must accept 1,000 code points); three tries per embedding batch; preparation budget 15 s plus a 1 s grace, and a record's fallback apply at most 3 s (inside the record outbox's 30 s processing budget); evidence items of at most 20 s under a 60 s lease, started only with at least 1 s of the step left, with preparation at most half and its grace at most a quarter of the item's budget; 1 s lock waits during apply; claims of up to 8 items, 2 per pair (never more than the claim's limit), walking 100 pairs, and a step stops after 1,000 walked pairs with nothing due; backoff caps of 5 minutes for outages and one hour for an item's own failures; status samples of 500 rows. The jobs Worker's once-a-minute tick runs `step` for up to 8 s after its other steps (15 + 7 + 10 + 5 + 5 s). The budgets sum to the 50 s tick budget and each is a hard end for its step's SQL and sends, so the retrieval step gets its whole 8 s on every tick, however busy the earlier steps are (it is skipped only with under 2 s of the tick left, more than an item's 1 s minimum plus its claim). With its 1 s cleanup and at most 5 s of teardown a tick ends by about 56 s, so ticks do not overlap and a tick holds only the one origin connection the topology allows it; were one to overrun anyway, leases and `SKIP LOCKED` keep overlapping ticks safe. A Ref's configured text is at most 1 MiB, so at most about 1,200 chunks; a larger one simply takes several attempts.

### Not covered here

There is no search, bundle, HTTP, CLI or MCP surface (STA-46, STA-47, STA-49), and generations are not started, verified, activated, rebuilt or backfilled by the worker (STA-48): a generation started after content was written projects only content delivered after it starts until STA-48's rebuild. Policy narrowing, the space and collection lifecycle and the Ref's authoritative position (a newer revision or extraction, or a deletion, stops preparation of the superseded text with `PROJECTION_INCOMPLETE`) are checked before every embedding call, retries included, and again by the storage triggers when checkpoints and chunks are written, but an embedding call already in flight when a narrower policy commits still completes; its result is then refused and the work runs again under the new policy. No production embedding provider is configured.
