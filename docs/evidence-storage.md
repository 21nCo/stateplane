# Evidence storage schema (STA-13 slice 1)

Migration `040_evidence_storage.sql` adds the Postgres tables for sources, original objects, upload grants and attempts, extraction versions, claims and typed links. STA-33 adds the source, original and upload services described in [Sources, originals and upload grants](#sources-originals-and-upload-grants-sta-33), STA-34 adds [text extraction](#text-extraction-sta-34), and STA-35 adds [claims](#claims-sta-35). No HTTP, CLI or MCP operation exposes them yet (STA-37), and links have no service yet (STA-36). The [v1 contract](../contracts/v1.md) §3 and §6 stay normative.

## Ownership and scope

Every row carries `space_id` and its owning `collection_id`, and every foreign-key path ends at `collections(space_id, collection_id)`. Sources and claims keep their canonical identity, revision and tombstone in the existing `entity_refs` table (migration 002). `sources` and `claims` hold their payload, with a kind-checked foreign key to that identity. A deferred constraint trigger on `entity_refs` makes sure that, at commit, each source identity has its source row and each live claim's current revision exists. Claim evidence may cite a source in another collection of the same space. A link endpoint must be in the link's space, and its owner must be one of its two endpoint collections. Citing or linking grants no access: service slices must authorize every endpoint and cited source against the caller's own collection grants.

| Table | Contents | Mutability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sources` | Locator (never proof of fetched bytes) and creating actor | Immutable; lifecycle via `entity_refs` |
| `original_objects` | One per source: `sha256`, `byte_size`, `mime_type`, `captured_at`, `observed_at`, `reference_only`, `object_key` (migration 041) | Immutable |
| `source_upload_grants` | Actor, collection, MIME, `max_bytes`, optional expected digest, expiry, then either revocation or finalization | Binding immutable; ends once, then final |
| `source_upload_attempts` | Staging blob key, observed digest, size and MIME, state, failure code, staging removal | Forward-only state; observations set once |
| `extraction_versions` | Parser, parser version, content, source offsets, cited original digest | Immutable |
| `claims` | Creating actor | Immutable; lifecycle via `entity_refs` |
| `claim_revisions` | Statement, attribution, the principal that recorded the revision (migration 042), observed and valid times, review status and reviewer | Immutable |
| `claim_evidence` | Cited source, optional extraction version and content range per revision | Immutable |
| `links` | Type, `restrict`/`detach` policy, typed `record`/`source`/`claim` endpoints | Tombstone only |

A source always has its original: `sources_original` is a deferred foreign key, so both rows commit together. A reference-only original has no digest or size, which discloses that no bytes were fetched. Its source must then have a locator, because nothing else points to the evidence; inserting a reference-only original for a source without one fails with `23514`, constraint `original_objects_reference_locator`. A stored original needs no locator. A stored original needs a digest, size and lowercase MIME type essence. Migration 041 adds `object_key`, the blob key of the original's bytes in the space's storage target: required for every new stored original, absent for a reference-only one (`original_objects_object_key`), and unique within a space, so two originals never share bytes. The digest identifies the bytes; the key locates them. An extraction version cites the exact original digest it parsed, so a reference-only source cannot have one. Versions and claim revisions start at 1 and have no gaps. A corrected extraction is the next version; earlier versions stay readable. Review produces a new claim revision. `accepted` and `rejected` require a reviewer and review time, and `unreviewed` is the default.

## Immutability and erasure

Triggers reject UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE on `sources`, `claims`, `original_objects`, `extraction_versions`, `claim_revisions` and `claim_evidence`, and DELETE and TRUNCATE on `links`. A source's locator and a source's or claim's creating principal, credential and time are provenance, so they never change; deletion and revision live in `entity_refs`. A link may change only from live to tombstoned, with the next revision and a tombstone time; its endpoints, type, policy and owner never change, and it is never resurrected. As with record events, one exception exists: the table owner may delete rows while running the `stateplane_purge_space` SECURITY DEFINER routine for that one `deleting` space. Setting `stateplane.erasing_space` as a non-owner role does not bypass the guard. The purge routine removes evidence rows before the records and identities they cite, and `PostgresSpaces` reports a space erased only when no evidence row remains. Original bytes and staging blobs live outside the database, so removing them is a separate retention step (STA-36). Removing the metadata alone is not proof that bytes or backups are gone.

## Upload ledger

The upload tables are a ledger that moves forward only, enforced by a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger on each. A grant's actor, collection, MIME type, `max_bytes`, expected digest, issue and expiry times never change. It ends exactly once, by revocation or by finalization with the committed source, and then no column changes. An attempt's staging key and start time never change. It may move from `receiving` to `verified`, and from either to `committed` or `failed`, but never back, and a committed or failed attempt keeps its state. The observed digest, size and MIME type, the failure code and the staging removal time are each set at most once, and a committed or failed attempt records nothing more except its staging removal. Only `updated_at` changes freely. A refused change fails with `23514` and constraint `source_upload_grants_transition` or `source_upload_attempts_transition`. Rows are never deleted outside space erasure; STA-33's upload service and reconciliation only advance them.

## Links and endpoint deletion

The database enforces the contract's "no live dangling link" rule. Tombstoning a record or `entity_refs` row while a live `restrict` link touches it fails with SQLSTATE `23001` (`restrict_violation`, constraint `links_restrict`). Mixed policies fail the same way. While only live `detach` links remain, the deletion fails with `23503`, constraint `links_detach`. The writer must tombstone those links, with their events and outbox entries, in the same transaction before the endpoint. Creating a link to a missing or tombstoned endpoint fails with `23503`, constraint `links_live_endpoint`.

No shipped surface creates links yet, so these errors cannot occur in production at this migration. The service layer does not map them yet either. If one reached `PostgresAuthority` today, a record delete would surface `23001` as the retryable `PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`, because the code is unmapped, and the record write path would turn any `23503`, including `links_detach` and `links_live_endpoint`, into `SCHEMA_CONFLICT`. STA-36 (STA-13 slice 5/6, links, deletion and retention) must do the following before any link-creating surface ships. It must map `links_restrict` to the non-retryable `LINK_RESTRICTED`, including in the batch item failure classifier. It must tombstone incident `detach` links, with their events and outbox entries, in the record and entity delete transaction before the endpoint. It must also stop collapsing `links_*` `23503` errors into `SCHEMA_CONFLICT`, and add a `PostgresAuthority` test that tells these outcomes apart.

A new link rewrites both live endpoint rows in place without changing any value. Because that is a real write, not only a row lock, it conflicts with the update that tombstones an endpoint at every isolation level, including a mix of levels. Under READ COMMITTED, whichever transaction runs second waits and then sees the other's commit. A REPEATABLE READ or SERIALIZABLE transaction whose snapshot predates the other's commit fails with `40001` instead, and its writer retries it like any serialization failure. The invariant therefore does not depend on `default_transaction_isolation`, and deleting an endpoint with no links succeeds at every level. The guard itself refuses an endpoint its rewrite cannot see, instead of leaving it to the foreign key. A foreign key checks only that the row exists, so it would accept an endpoint that a concurrent transaction created and tombstoned while the link waited on its other endpoint. An endpoint created by a transaction that has not committed when the link reaches it is therefore refused too. Link creation also serializes with ordinary updates to its endpoint rows, and with other link creations on the same endpoint, until it commits. The two endpoints are rewritten in a fixed order (kind, collection, id, in byte order), so links created in opposite directions between the same endpoints queue rather than deadlock. A writer that creates links still treats `40001` and `40P01` as retryable, because its other statements in the same transaction can take row locks in any order. The rewrite touches only `revision`, which no column-specific trigger watches, so it emits no record events and changes no projection. It does queue the deferred `entity_refs_payload` check for a source or claim endpoint, which the unchanged row passes. The guard functions run with owner rights, so operational roles can keep deleting records without link privileges.

## Bounds

These are initial integrity backstops, not measured provider limits. Extraction content, its offsets JSON and claim statements are each capped at 1,048,576 UTF-8 bytes, the same bound as canonical record data. Locators are capped at 4,096 bytes, attribution at 1,024, parser names, versions and link types at 256, staging keys at 1,024 and failure codes at 64. MIME types follow the RFC 6838 essence form with at most 127 characters per part. STA-33 records the text, upload and grant-lifetime bounds [below](#bounds-for-sources-and-uploads), and STA-34 the supported extraction MIME types, offset units and [extraction bounds](#bounds-for-extraction).

## Upgrade, restore and grants

Migration 040 creates empty tables and adds triggers to `records` and `entity_refs`. It scans no existing rows, so a populated upgrade needs no drained-traffic flag. It does take `SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE` locks on `collections`, `records` and `entity_refs` until the migration commits. The migrator bounds that wait to five seconds. A busy record writer makes it fail and roll back without any 040 objects, and it is safe to retry. Like the rest of the migration history, 040 is forward-only: "down" means that rollback, not a reverse script. Triggers and foreign keys are post-data objects, so `pg_restore` loads rows first and then recreates the guards. The restore smoke checks that a restored original still rejects updates and that a restored `restrict` link still blocks deleting its record, then reapplies these grants to a nonowner role and reads every evidence table through it. Space deletion reads every evidence table to prove erasure before it publishes `deleted`, so the cell operational role needs `SELECT` on them now. After migration 040 and after every restore, run `pnpm db:grant-evidence` through the direct administrative connection with `STATEPLANE_CELL_ADMIN_URL` and the protected inventory's `STATEPLANE_CELL_ROLE`. It grants `SELECT` on the nine evidence tables, plus the STA-33 and STA-34 writes listed [below](#grants-and-verification), and reads each privilege back. Without it a nonowner role's purge still commits, but the deletion fails with `42501` before publication and stays retryable. Erasure itself needs no write privilege: it uses the purge routine. Evidence writes get their grants with the service slices that perform them.

Migration 041 adds `original_objects.object_key` with its checks and a unique partial index. No service wrote originals before it, but fixture rows may exist and originals are immutable, so `original_objects_object_key` is added `NOT VALID`: it binds every new row and scans none. A stored original from before 041 has no key and reads as missing bytes (`PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`). It takes an `ACCESS EXCLUSIVE` lock on `original_objects`, and creating the `source_upload_attempts_expiry` constraint trigger takes a `SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE` lock on `source_upload_attempts`; both are held until the migration commits, so upload writers wait for it. The migrator bounds each lock wait to five seconds like 040, and a busy source or upload writer makes it roll back and be safe to retry.

Migration 043 adds the `claim_revisions_authority` constraint trigger and its function. It reads and changes no rows; creating the trigger takes a `SHARE ROW EXCLUSIVE` lock on `claim_revisions` until the migration commits, bounded to five seconds like 040 to 042, so a busy claim writer makes it roll back and be safe to retry. The function runs with the caller's rights and reads only `space_credentials`, `spaces`, `collection_grants`, `collections` and `claim_evidence`, which the claim service's own final checks already read through the cell role, so it needs no new grant; the nonowner lifecycle test runs it through such a role. A trigger is a post-data object, so `pg_restore` loads revisions before recreating it.

## Sources, originals and upload grants (STA-33)

`PostgresSources` (in `@stateplane/postgres`) is the application service for sources. `get` and `content` need `sources:read`. `ingestText`, `ingestReference`, `createUpload`, `upload`, `finalizeUpload` and `revokeUpload` need `sources:write`. Every call is admitted for the caller's own grant on the owning collection. Its transaction checks space, collection, policy version, placement and grant at the start and again before COMMIT, and rechecks the credential after the last statement, like record writes. A transaction that verifies an upload attempt or commits a grant's source is also checked against the grant's expiry as it commits, by migration 041's deferred `source_upload_attempts_expiry` trigger, so the final authority checks cannot carry either one past expiry. The transports reach these operations in STA-37.

### Access

A source is read only through its owning collection. A source ID from another collection, asked through a readable one, is `NOT_FOUND`. An unreadable collection fails with `FORBIDDEN` whether or not the source exists, so it reveals nothing. `sources:write` alone grants no reads. A `readOnly` space or collection refuses new sources and grants with `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`, but a retry of a committed ingestion still replays, as for record receipts. A `suspended`, `deleting` or `deleted` space refuses everything.

`content` evaluates authorization when the response starts. It authorizes the caller and reads the source, opens the original, and then checks the caller's authority and the source again before it returns the body. A credential revocation, a collection grant or policy change, a lifecycle change or a placement change while the original is being opened is refused, and the opened body is cancelled. A body already returned is not cut off by a later change.

### Original bytes

Original bytes live behind the `BlobStore` port (`@stateplane/application`). `R2BlobStore` (`@stateplane/storage`) implements it over the cell's R2 binding, `ORIGINALS`. Each original is stored under a key of its own, `originals/<base64url(spaceId)>/<grant>/<attempt>`, named after the upload attempt that promoted it and recorded in `original_objects.object_key` when that attempt commits. Identical bytes in two sources are stored twice; no object is shared, so removing one attempt's bytes can never affect another source (below). A write streams its body through a check that counts and hashes every chunk. It stores nothing unless the body has exactly the declared length and, when one is expected, the expected SHA-256. R2's put is atomic, so a refused or interrupted body leaves no object. In Workers the checked stream passes through `FixedLengthStream`, because R2 needs a known length. Every original is written with R2's `sha256` option, so R2 verifies the bytes and keeps the checksum on the object. Nothing ever writes different bytes under an original's key. `content` refuses an object whose size or stored checksum differs from the record, and it streams the bytes through a second check that errors the stream at the end if their digest or length differs. A consumer must treat that stream error as corruption. The published `@superfunctions/storage-r2@0.2.0` adapter is not used: it reaches R2 through the S3 API with account keys, not the regional binding, and its conformance is still pending ([package qualification](package-qualification.md)).

### Ingestion

- **Text.** Well-formed Unicode of 1 byte to 1 MiB in UTF-8, with a `text/*` MIME type (default `text/plain`), an optional locator and an optional UTC `capturedAt` that is not in the future. A string longer than 1,048,576 UTF-16 code units is refused before it is encoded, since UTF-8 needs at least one byte for each. The bytes are hashed in memory and pass through the same ledger as an upload: a 60-second one-shot grant with the exact size and digest, and an attempt recorded as `verified` before any byte is written. The staging key is never written.
- **Reference only.** A locator of at most 4,096 bytes is required, and a MIME type is optional. The locator is stored and never fetched. The original has no digest or size, which discloses that no bytes are held.
- **Upload.** `createUpload` issues a grant bound to the space, collection, principal and credential, one MIME type, a `maxBytes` limit, an optional expected SHA-256 and an expiry. `upload` declares the exact size, MIME type and an optional digest. A declaration that differs from the grant is refused before any attempt or byte exists. A grant takes a new attempt only while none of its attempts is `receiving` or `verified`; the grant row lock serializes that check, so a grant stages at most one live attempt of at most `maxBytes`. A failed attempt does not block a retry, and an upload whose verification transaction fails for any reason fails its own attempt before it returns, so the same grant accepts the retry. A writer that died mid-stream leaves its attempt `receiving` until reconciliation, so its caller revokes the grant and asks for a new one. The body then streams into `staging/<space>/<grant>/<attempt>`. It is counted and hashed, a `text/*` body must decode as UTF-8, and the write is cut off at the grant's expiry. A grant serves only its own credential; another credential gets `NOT_FOUND`. `finalizeUpload` names a verified attempt, an optional locator and an optional `capturedAt`. It copies the staged bytes to the attempt's original key, with R2 rechecking the digest, before the source metadata commits. Then it removes the staging object.

Request objects must be plain data objects, with `Object.prototype` or no prototype, whose own members are all enumerable data fields with documented string keys. Every other shape is `INVALID_ARGUMENT`: an accessor (which is never invoked), a symbol, non-enumerable or unknown member, an inherited field, an array or another prototype. A field given as `undefined` is supplied, not omitted, as `contracts/v1.md` defines for envelopes, so it is refused too, required or optional. A proxy, whether transparent, throwing or revoked, is refused with `INVALID_ARGUMENT` before any reflective read can run its traps, as `contracts/v1.md` requires of in-process adapters, and so is an upload body that is a proxy. A runtime without trap-free proxy detection refuses every in-process request object. An optional field takes its default only when absent: a supplied `null`, for example `expiresInSeconds: null`, is refused rather than read as the 300-second default. The collection is authorized first, and a refused request changes nothing.

Every decision on a request uses operations captured when the module loads, as `contracts/v1.md` requires of in-process inputs. Own keys and the allowed and required field lists are read by own index, never through `Array.prototype[Symbol.iterator]`, `includes` or `some`. Text is checked for NUL and lone surrogates by reading its UTF-16 code units, MIME essences are cut, trimmed and lowercased with captured `String` methods and matched with a captured `RegExp.prototype.exec`, digests are matched the same way, integers use a captured `Number.isSafeInteger`, and derived source IDs serialize their parts with a captured `JSON.stringify` applied to each string. A later change to any of those cannot drop a supplied digest, admit an unknown key, a missing field, an invalid MIME type or a fractional integer, or merge two source identities. Node's `Buffer` and `crypto` internals are trusted as the runtime provides them.

MIME verification compares media-type essences (lowercase `type/subtype`, parameters dropped) and checks UTF-8 for `text/*`. It does not sniff binary formats.

Each ingestion is idempotent without a receipt table, which references records only. A text or reference source's ID is derived from `(operation, credential, idempotency key)`, and an upload's from its grant. A retry finds the committed source and replays it with `replayed: true`. A different text, locator, MIME type or `capturedAt` under the same key fails with `IDEMPOTENCY_MISMATCH`. So does the same key in another collection, after that collection is authorized for the caller. An omitted `capturedAt` is the observation time and matches only another omission. Concurrent retries serialize on the source identity and commit one source. A replay does not expire. Issuing a grant is not idempotent: an unused grant simply expires.

A removal must never delete bytes a committed source relies on, even if its delete reaches R2 long after it was sent, for example after the remover's database session or Worker ended. Because no two attempts share an original key, this needs no lock. An attempt's key is referenced only by the `original_objects` row its own commit inserts, and only while the attempt is `verified` can that commit happen. Its bytes are removed only after a transaction that left the attempt `failed` has committed: the revocation, the failed finalize or upload's cleanup, or reconciliation. `committed` and `failed` are both final, so a delete, however late, targets bytes no commit uses or will use. A transition that rolls back leaves its attempt verified and its bytes intact. After the copy, and before the metadata transaction begins, finalize heads the object and requires the attempt's size and checksum; this head is bounded by the grant's deadline and holds no lock. If the object is missing, finalize fails with retryable `PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`, the attempt stays verified, and a retry copies the bytes again. Checking outside the transaction is enough: only a removal deletes the key, and only after the attempt's failure has committed, so the metadata commit, which locks the attempt and requires it to be `verified`, cannot commit once any removal of its bytes has started. A promotion that finds the object already present with a matching size and checksum, from an earlier finalize of the same attempt, does not copy it again. One that finds it missing copies it again from the attempt's own staging object, which stays until the attempt ends.

A revocation can fail an attempt while its finalize is still copying bytes, so that copy can land after the revocation removed the original, and any write reported as failed at its deadline can land after its writer's cleanup. Four steps keep such bytes from outliving their failed attempt:

1. **Writes end at the grant's deadline.** When an upload or finalize reads its grant row, it takes a local monotonic reading (`performance.now`) *before* sending the query and adds the remaining lifetime the database computes. The deadline is therefore never later than `expires_at`, and nothing after the read restarts the countdown. No write starts once the deadline has passed. After it, the body errors at its next chunk and at its end, so R2 can only store a body that had already ended before the deadline. A write the store has not confirmed by the deadline is reported as `deadline` at once, without waiting for R2's answer, and the caller treats its key as possibly written.
2. **A failed finalize removes its original again.** A finalize that fails for any reason, including a `deadline` write, removes its attempt's original once the attempt's failure has committed. A `deadline` write is reported before R2 answers, so a copy can still land after this removal.
3. **Reconciliation removes it once more.** A failed attempt that held a digest keeps `staging_removed_at` empty, even after its staging object is deleted, so it stays due for reconciliation. Reconciliation removes its original again after the grant's grace period, and only then records `staging_removed_at`. That covers a finalize that died or lost its connection between its copy and its own removal.
4. **The sweep finds whatever lands later.** No R2 limit we can cite bounds when R2 acknowledges a PUT whose complete body it received, so a staging write or a copy can land after reconciliation recorded its attempt as settled. The ledger then no longer lists it, so `SourceUploadReconciler.sweep` lists the bucket itself instead (below). Every key a write can land at stays discoverable there until it is deleted, so this cleanup does not depend on timing. The 300-second grace only makes the sweep rarely needed. Measuring R2's settlement of a put aborted after its body ended is still part of the Cloudflare Preview acceptance.

Every sources transaction runs at `READ COMMITTED`, whatever the session's `default_transaction_isolation`. Each lock-then-read step, such as the grant and attempt row locks and the source-identity lock that serializes retries, must read rows committed before its lock was granted. A snapshot taken before the lock could miss them. A session that may still hold an open transaction or an unanswered statement, because BEGIN got no reply, a read timed out or COMMIT went unacknowledged, is discarded rather than returned to the pool, as `PostgresAuthority.transaction` does. A read timeout or lost session before COMMIT is sent is reported as retryable `PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`, so a finalize leaves its attempt `verified` for a retry. An upload's verification instead releases its attempt, as the failure table describes, because its consumed body cannot be verified again.

### Reconciliation

The cell jobs Worker's once-a-minute tick runs `SourceUploadReconciler.step` after projection dispatch and outbox reconciliation, in pages of 25 for up to 10 seconds. Each step receives what remains of those 10 seconds and starts no attempt after it; a page cut short still reports its due attempts, so the next tick continues it. Each R2 call and each database transaction during cleanup is bounded to 2 seconds or the remaining budget, whichever is shorter, and none starts after the budget: the deadline is checked before each call is issued. For a transaction the bound covers waiting for a connection, its statements, its COMMIT and, after an error, its ROLLBACK. A session cut off mid-statement or mid-ROLLBACK, or whose ROLLBACK could not start before the budget ended, is discarded, and a connection that arrives late is released. A call that times out fails its attempt's settlement like any other error, and the delete or update may land later, which is safe because its attempt is final and every cleanup step repeats safely. A page selects attempts whose staging is not yet removed and whose grant expired more than the 300-second grace period ago, through the `source_upload_attempts_staged` partial index. Each attempt settles in its own transaction under `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. A `receiving` or `verified` attempt fails with `abandoned`. For any attempt that held a digest and did not commit, its original is removed as above. Then the staging object is deleted and `staging_removed_at` recorded. This covers writers that died mid-stream, finalizes that promoted bytes but never committed, copies that landed after a revocation, and staging that a committed or failed request could not delete. One attempt's failure does not stop the others. A failing attempt's `updated_at` is advanced in its own transaction, so it moves behind every due attempt not tried since; once the budget has ended no such transaction starts, and the attempt is retried on a later tick. Attempts that keep failing therefore cannot hold the oldest page: each due attempt older than a healthy one is tried at most once before it, so the healthy one is reached within one tick per page of such attempts. The step reports all of them in one `AggregateError`, which the tick raises with any dispatch or outbox reconciliation error, and the failing attempt is retried on a later tick. A tick without the `ORIGINALS` binding fails before it opens a database connection.

The tick then runs `SourceUploadReconciler.sweep` for one page of 100 keys for up to 5 seconds. It lists the bucket after a cursor stored in the bucket at `reconcile/sweep-cursor`, finds each `staging/` and `originals/` key's attempt through the `(space_id, staging_key)` unique index, and deletes the staging and original bytes of `failed` attempts and the staging bytes of `committed` ones. Both states are final, so these deletes are safe however late they land. A committed original, a `receiving` or `verified` attempt's bytes and any key the ledger does not know are kept. Each R2 call is bounded as above. The cursor body is read through a reader the sweep owns, one bounded read at a time, so a cursor body that stalls or fails is cancelled through R2 and released before the sweep ends. Deletes stop when four fifths of the budget have passed, so the cursor write still fits when deletes are slow. The cursor advances past the keys the page examined, and a finished pass starts over. A lost cursor or a failed delete only repeats work on the next pass. Its errors join the tick's like `step`'s. Attempts of a live grant are never touched. The upload ledger is never deleted outside space erasure, and erasing a space's original and staging bytes is STA-36's retention step.

## Text extraction (STA-34)

`PostgresSources.extract`, `correctExtraction` and `extraction` turn a source into immutable, versioned text. `extraction` needs `sources:read`. `extract` and `correctExtraction` return the source's text, so they need both `sources:write` and `sources:read` on the owning collection; a writer without read access gets `FORBIDDEN`. Each capability is checked at the start and again before COMMIT of every transaction, and the credential is rechecked last, as for other source calls. A source is reached only through its owning collection, as for `get`. A `readOnly` space or collection refuses a new version with `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE` but still replays a committed one. The transports reach these operations in STA-37.

### Parser contract and supported types

The contract is in `@stateplane/application`: `TextParser`, `parserFor` and `checkExtraction`. A parser has a name, a version and an explicit list of MIME essences. It receives the whole verified original and returns content and source offsets. Each version records its parser, parser version and the digest of the original it parsed.

Source offsets are segments `{ start, end, sourceStart, sourceEnd }`. `start` and `end` are a half-open range of the extraction's content in Unicode code points. That is the unit of Postgres `char_length` and `substring`, so a range selects the same text in every client language. `sourceStart` and `sourceEnd` are a half-open byte range of the original. Every extraction, parsed or corrected, must pass `checkExtraction`:

- the content is well-formed Unicode without NUL, within the content bound (Postgres text cannot hold NUL);
- there are at most 4,096 segments;
- each content range is nonempty, lies within the content and starts at or after the previous segment's end;
- each byte range lies within the original. It may be empty, for text that has no source position.

`checkExtraction` refuses a hole, an accessor or a non-object segment like any other malformed one. `extract` checks every parser's output itself, so a parser that skips the check cannot store an extraction outside the contract.

`stateplane.text` version `1` is the only parser. It accepts `text/plain`, `text/markdown`, `text/csv` and `text/tab-separated-values`. It decodes the original as UTF-8 and keeps it verbatim apart from one leading byte order mark. It does no newline, whitespace or Unicode normalization, so its single segment maps the content to the original byte for byte (`sourceStart` is 3 after a byte order mark). Markdown, CSV and TSV are kept as written; their markup is not interpreted. Every other type is refused, with no format sniffing and no fallback. That includes `text/html` and `text/xml`, whose markup would need a real parser, and every binary format. A stored MIME type is an essence without parameters, so no charset can be declared. `text/*` uploads are already checked as UTF-8 while streaming, and the parser checks again.

### Versions

`extract` writes the next version using the parser for the source's MIME type. If that parser and version already produced a version of this source, `extract` replays it with `replayed: true`, even after later corrections. So extracting is idempotent per parser version and never undoes a correction; a new parser version writes a new version.

`correctExtraction` takes `{ baseVersion, content, sourceOffsets }` and writes the version after `baseVersion`, which must be the latest. The correction is recorded with parser `stateplane.correction`, version `1`; parser names starting with `stateplane.` are reserved for these built-in producers. If a later version already exists, a correction identical to the version after its base, from the same credential, replays it, so an unacknowledged commit can be retried. Any other correction then fails with `REVISION_CONFLICT`, and the caller should correct the latest version instead. A `baseVersion` beyond the latest is `NOT_FOUND`, and so is any correction of a source that was never extracted.

Versions start at 1 and have no gaps. Every earlier version stays readable through `extraction(…, version)`; without a version, `extraction` reads the latest. Each view reports `version`, `latestVersion` and `previousVersion`. Versions are immutable (migration 040), so a correction never changes text that a claim cites.

Writers on one source serialize on a transaction-scoped advisory lock, then read the latest version. Concurrent extractions therefore commit one version and the others replay it, and of concurrent corrections to one base, one commits and the others conflict. Unlike a row lock on `entity_refs`, the advisory lock needs no table privilege. Source deletion (STA-36) must take the same lock before it tombstones a source, so no version commits after its source is deleted.

### Refusals

The original is read and parsed between two transactions. Nothing is written until the parser has accepted the whole original, so a refused or failed extraction leaves no row. Correction requests follow the request-shape rules of the source requests above. `sourceOffsets` must be a plain array, with no holes or extra members, whose items are plain objects with exactly the four integer fields.

| Input | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Unsupported MIME type, reference-only source, or original over 1 MiB | `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, decided from the recorded metadata before any byte is read |
| Original that is not well-formed UTF-8 or contains NUL | `INVALID_ARGUMENT` after the read |
| Malformed correction: request shape, offsets out of range, out of order or over 4,096 segments, or content that is invalid or over the bound | `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, after the collection is authorized |
| Original missing, or its size or stored checksum differs from the record, or its streamed digest or length differs | `PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`, not retryable; an opened body is cancelled without waiting for the store to acknowledge it |
| Read of the original fails or does not finish within 10 seconds | `PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`, retryable; the body is cancelled |
| Caller's authority changes while the original is read | The final transaction's refusal (`FORBIDDEN`, `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`, …) |

### Bounds for extraction

These are initial bounds with the evidence for each, not measured capacity. STA-19 measures deployed limits.

| Bound | Value | Evidence and purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original size | Up to 1,048,576 bytes | The content column's bound (migration 040) and the text ingestion bound. Verbatim text needs as many content bytes as source bytes. The original is held in one buffer and decoded once, well within a Worker's 128 MB memory. A larger original is refused from its recorded size, before any read. |
| Content | Up to 1,048,576 UTF-8 bytes | The `extraction_versions.content` check in migration 040 |
| Segments | Up to 4,096 per version | A segment with safe-integer offsets is at most about 120 bytes of `jsonb` text, so 4,096 stay under 500 KB. That is within migration 040's 1 MiB check on `source_offsets`, so the check never refuses an extraction that `checkExtraction` accepted. |
| Original read | 10 s | So that a stalled R2 read cannot hold the request. Not measured: reading 1 MiB through the cell's own R2 binding is expected to take far less. |

## Claims (STA-35)

`PostgresClaims` (in `@stateplane/postgres`) records explicit claims: attributed statements with evidence references, valid and observed time, and review. `create` needs `claims:write`. `revise` needs `claims:write` and `claims:read`, and `review` needs the privileged `claims:review` and `claims:read`, because both act on content the caller must be able to read. `get` and `list` need `claims:read`. Each capability is checked at the start and again before COMMIT of the call's one transaction. The identity provider is asked last; after it answers, one statement checks, against one clock reading and with no other await in between, that the credential is live, that its owning-collection grant holds every capability the call needs, and that it holds `sources:read` on every cited collection the call relied on. A grant that lapses while an earlier check or the provider runs therefore refuses the call. COMMIT is a separate round trip, so a write by a regional key is checked once more as it commits, by migration 043's deferred `claim_revisions_authority` trigger: the key must still be live and issued by the space's owner, its owning-collection grant must still hold the capabilities of the write the revision records (revision 1 `claims:write`; a later revision `claims:write` and `claims:read`; a review `claims:review` and `claims:read`), and it must still hold `sources:read` on every collection any revision of the claim cites. A grant or key that lapses after the final statement therefore rolls back the claim, its revision, its evidence and its current-revision pointer together, and the call is `FORBIDDEN`. A revision recorded by an owner session names no regional key, so the identity provider decides it as before. `get` and `list` write nothing; they return what the final statement authorized. A claim is read only through its owning collection: a claim ID from another collection is `NOT_FOUND`. Request shapes follow the [source request rules](#ingestion): plain data objects with documented fields, decided with intrinsics captured at module load, and refused only after the collection is authorized. A `readOnly` space or collection refuses new claims and revisions with `SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`, but a retry of a committed create or revision still replays. A `readOnly` collection also refuses review, even a replay, because the authority fence treats `claims:review` as a write. A `suspended`, `deleting` or `deleted` space refuses everything. The transports reach these operations in STA-37.

Claims never write records. The service writes only `entity_refs` (kind `claim`), `claims`, `claim_revisions` and `claim_evidence`; it emits no record event and no outbox entry, and nothing consolidates, extracts or applies claims to records. An accepted claim is an attributed, reviewed assertion beside the record, not a change to it.

### Lifecycle

- **Create** takes `{ idempotencyKey, statement, attributedTo, observedAt?, validFrom?, validUntil?, evidence? }` and writes revision 1, `unreviewed`. `attributedTo` names who asserts the statement, such as a person, publication or agent, and is separate from the principals that record it: `createdBy` is the principal that created the claim, the same on every revision, and `recordedBy` the principal that recorded the revision shown. `observedAt` is when the asserted fact was observed and may not be in the future; it stays null when not stated. `validFrom` and `validUntil` bound when the statement holds, may be in the future, and must be in order. The three times are UTC instants with at most millisecond precision, the precision views report, so a view always shows the stored time; a finer time is `INVALID_ARGUMENT` (trailing zeros are not precision). A leap second (second 60, which record values accept) is also `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, because `timestamptz` cannot store it as itself: it would become the next second, or with a fraction fail outright. The claim ID is derived from the credential and idempotency key. A retry with the same content replays revision 1 with `replayed: true`; different content, or the same key in another collection after that collection is authorized, is `IDEMPOTENCY_MISMATCH`.
- **Revise** takes `{ baseRevision, …content }` without an idempotency key and writes the revision after `baseRevision`, which must be the latest, recorded by the caller, who need not be the claim's creator. It replaces the whole content and is `unreviewed`, whatever the base's review status. If a later revision exists, an identical unreviewed revision after the base, from the same credential, replays it, so an unacknowledged commit can be retried; anything else is `REVISION_CONFLICT` with the latest revision. A base after the latest is `NOT_FOUND`.
- **Review** takes `{ baseRevision, status }`, where `status` is `accepted` or `rejected`, and writes the revision after the latest with the same content and evidence, the reviewer's principal as both `review.reviewedBy` and `recordedBy`, and the review time. The content and evidence are copied from the stored revision in SQL, never through a view, so a review changes no value, including a time stored with microseconds. Replay and conflict follow the revise rule, comparing the later revision with the stored base. Only review sets a review status: create and revise refuse a status field like any unknown field. A rejected or superseded revision stays readable, and a later revision can review again.

Every revision is immutable (migration 040) and stores the principal that recorded it (migration 042, whose check binds every new revision to a recorder and a review revision to its reviewer; it adds the column without a backfill because no service wrote claims before it, so only a fixture revision stored earlier reports `recordedBy: null`). `get` reads any revision of a live claim, the latest when none is given. Each view reports `revision`, `latestRevision` and `previousRevision`. Writers on one claim, including a create and its retries, serialize on a transaction advisory lock on the claim identity, then read the latest revision, so concurrent revisions to one base commit one and the others conflict.

### Conflicting claims

Nothing decides between claims. Reviewing a claim changes no other claim, and an accepted, a rejected and an unreviewed claim about the same thing all stay readable. `list` returns the current revisions of live claims in the collection, in claim ID order and whatever their status, in pages of up to 25. `citing: { collectionId, sourceId }` keeps the claims whose current revision cites that source, which lists the competing claims about it, and `status` keeps one review status. `after` takes the previous page's `next`, a claim ID. It is a key boundary in an already authorized collection and carries no other state, so unlike record query cursors it needs no signature: any value only moves the start of the page.

### Evidence and access

Evidence is up to 32 items, each citing a live source as `{ collectionId, sourceId }`, optionally a version of its extraction (`extractionVersion`), and optionally a nonempty half-open range of that version's content (`start`, `end`), in the same Unicode code points as extraction segments. Extraction versions are immutable, so a cited range always selects the same text. A missing source or version in a readable collection is `NOT_FOUND`, and a range beyond the content is `INVALID_ARGUMENT`.

Citing grants no access, and declassification is not supported. Two rules carry that:

- **Citing.** The caller needs `sources:read` on every cited collection, in addition to the claim capability. Otherwise the call is `FORBIDDEN` before any source is looked up, so it reveals nothing about whether the source, or the collection, exists.
- **Reading.** A claim is visible only to a caller who holds `sources:read` on every collection that any of its revisions cites. Otherwise `get`, `revise` and `review` are `NOT_FOUND`, exactly as for a claim that does not exist, and `list` omits the claim. `list` with `citing` an unreadable collection is `FORBIDDEN`. The rule covers every revision, not only the current one: a statement written from a cited source can carry that source's content, so revising the citation away does not make the claim visible to more callers. A claim without evidence needs no source access. The owner reads every live collection; a claim citing a deleted collection is `NOT_FOUND` for every caller, the owner included, and `list` omits it.

Visibility is decided in the call's transaction from one statement that reads the revision and the collections all revisions cite, so a revision committed concurrently cannot add a citation the decision did not see. Each cited collection the call relies on is then fenced like the claim's own collection: its grant, lifecycle, policy version and placement are checked and share-locked, checked again before COMMIT, and included in the final grant statement. `list` reads one claim past the page to decide `next`, and admits and fences that claim like the page's own, so a grant that lapses on a collection only it cites refuses the call too. A grant revoked or lapsing, or a collection deleted, while the call runs therefore refuses it.

A claim in collection A can thus never disclose a source of collection B, or text derived from it, to a caller who cannot read B's sources. Links are STA-36, and must not widen this: a link to a claim grants no access to it. No operation deletes a source yet; STA-36 decides what source deletion does to the claims that cite it.
